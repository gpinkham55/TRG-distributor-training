Gaskets and seals are cut from sheet and roll materials. The material decides what the gasket can seal: which media, how hot, and how much pressure. This module covers the four main families of gasket material and how to pick one. Module 11 covers flanges and installation; Module 12 covers cutting and pricing.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Describe the common elastomer sheet rubbers and where each fits.
2. Read a sheet rubber spec: polymer, durometer, thickness, width, and any cloth insertion.
3. Explain where compressed fiber sheet, PTFE, ePTFE, and flexible graphite fit.
4. Use temperature, pressure, and media to choose a gasket material.
5. Give examples of gasket materials in each of the four industries.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Old gaskets, especially in steam lines and older plants, may contain asbestos. Never cut, grind, or sand an old gasket a customer brings in. Handle it as little as possible and ask your mentor how your branch deals with it.

## 10.1 Elastomer Sheet Rubber

Elastomer sheet is solid rubber in rolls or sheets. It seals well at low pressure and moderate temperature and is used for flange gaskets, pads, seals, and wear strips.

### Reading a sheet rubber spec

| Spec | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Polymer | The base rubber | Neoprene |
| Durometer | Hardness on the Shore A scale; higher is harder | 60 Shore A (a common gasket hardness) |
| Thickness | Usually in fractions of an inch | 1/16 in, 1/8 in, 1/4 in |
| Width and form | Roll width or sheet size | 36 in or 48 in wide rolls |
| Cloth insertion | Fabric layers inside the rubber for strength | 1-ply or 2-ply insertion |
| Grade | Commercial, specification (ASTM D2000), or FDA | FDA white nitrile for food |

### Common elastomers

Temperature ranges are approximate. Always check the maker's data sheet for the exact grade.

| Elastomer | Good with | Poor with | Approx. range (°F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural rubber and red SBR | Water, air, abrasion, general use | Oils, ozone, heat | -20 to 170 |
| Neoprene (CR) | Moderate oil, weather, ozone, refrigerants | Strong acids, ketones | -40 to 220 |
| Nitrile (Buna-N, NBR) | Oils, fuels, hydraulic fluid | Ozone and sunlight, ketones | -20 to 225 |
| EPDM | Water, steam, weather, many dilute acids and caustics | Petroleum oils and fuels | -40 to 300 |
| FKM (often sold as Viton) | Fuels, oils, many acids, high heat | Ketones, steam, some amines | -10 to 400 |
| Silicone | Very wide temperature range, food contact | Abrasion, tearing, steam at pressure | -80 to 450 |
| Gum rubber | Abrasion, cushioning; pure natural rubber | Oils, heat | -20 to 160 |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Rolls of red SBR, black neoprene, white FDA nitrile, and orange silicone sheet on a rack]

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Sheet rubber often goes by color at the counter: "red rubber," "black neoprene," "white food grade." Color is a clue, not a spec. Confirm the polymer and durometer on the label before you cut.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Rubber gaskets are for low pressure and moderate heat. For steam, high heat, or high pressure, step up to compressed fiber, PTFE, or graphite.

## 10.2 Compressed Fiber Sheet

Compressed fiber sheet is made of fibers (aramid, glass, carbon, or mineral) bound together with a rubber binder such as nitrile. It is the everyday gasket material for plant piping: water, low-pressure steam, oils, air, and many mild chemicals.

| Feature | What to know |
|---|---|
| Binder | Nitrile binder for oils and general service; SBR or other binders for other media |
| Thickness | 1/16 in is the most common for flat, smooth flanges; 1/8 in for older or uneven flanges |
| Strength | Much higher pressure and temperature limits than rubber sheet |
| Limits | Strong acids and caustics attack many grades; check the maker's chart |

### The P×T chart

Gasket makers rate fiber, PTFE, and graphite sheet with a **P×T chart**: a graph of pressure against temperature, split into zones. Plot the customer's pressure and temperature. The zone tells you whether the material is suitable, needs an engineering check, or is not suitable.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A gasket maker's P×T chart with three zones shaded and one service point plotted]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Thinner is usually better. A 1/16 in fiber gasket holds more pressure and creeps less than a 1/8 in gasket of the same material, as long as the flanges are flat and smooth.

## 10.3 PTFE, ePTFE, and Flexible Graphite

### PTFE

PTFE resists almost every chemical. Plain (virgin) PTFE creeps under bolt load, so most PTFE gasket sheet is **filled** to resist creep.

| Type | What it is | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Filled PTFE (silica) | PTFE with silica filler | Strong acids |
| Filled PTFE (barium sulfate) | PTFE with barium sulfate filler | Strong caustics, and many acids |
| Filled PTFE (glass microspheres) | PTFE with hollow glass spheres; more compressible | Lower bolt loads, plastic and lined flanges |
| ePTFE (expanded) | Soft, stretched PTFE that conforms to the flange | Uneven, damaged, glass-lined, or FRP flanges |
| PTFE envelope | A PTFE jacket over a rubber or fiber insert | Chemical service where only the PTFE touches the media |

### Flexible graphite

Flexible graphite handles very high temperatures and is a standard choice for steam and hot oil. It often has a thin stainless steel insert for strength and handling.

- Good with steam, hot water, heat transfer oils, and most chemicals.
- Poor with strong oxidizers such as concentrated nitric acid.
- The surface marks easily. Handle it by the edges and don't fold it.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Sheets of filled PTFE, ePTFE, and flexible graphite with a tanged stainless insert, side by side]

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Graphite and some PTFE grades leave a mark when you touch them and lose their seal if creased. Store them flat and handle them by the edges, at the rack and in the shop.

## 10.4 Choosing a Gasket Material

Start with the media, then check temperature and pressure, then the flange.

| Service | First choices | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Cold water, air, low pressure | Red SBR, neoprene, EPDM, compressed fiber | Nothing special |
| Hot water and low-pressure steam | EPDM (lower pressure), compressed fiber | Nitrile, neoprene, natural rubber |
| Higher-pressure steam | Flexible graphite, high-temperature compressed fiber | Rubber sheet |
| Oils, fuels, hydraulic fluid | Nitrile, FKM, nitrile-bound compressed fiber | EPDM, natural rubber |
| Strong acids | Silica-filled PTFE, ePTFE, FKM for some acids | Most rubber, most fiber sheet |
| Strong caustics | Barium sulfate-filled PTFE, EPDM at lower temperatures | Silica-filled PTFE |
| Food and beverage | FDA white nitrile, EPDM, or silicone; PTFE | Anything not marked food grade |
| Glass-lined, plastic, or FRP flanges | ePTFE, soft rubber | Hard sheet that needs high bolt load |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: For steam, chemicals, and anything above low pressure, confirm the material with the maker's chart and P×T rating. If the service is outside what you know, stop and ask your mentor.

### Industry applications

| Industry | Common gasket jobs | Usual materials |
|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Steam headers, digester flanges, bleach plant piping, paper machine seals | Graphite, compressed fiber, ePTFE, EPDM |
| Agriculture | Pump and tank lid gaskets, irrigation flanges, equipment covers | EPDM, nitrile, neoprene, fiber sheet |
| Chemical | Acid and caustic lines, lined vessels, tank manways | Filled PTFE, ePTFE, FKM, EPDM |
| Heavy equipment | Hydraulic tank and manifold gaskets, covers, pads | Nitrile, neoprene, fiber sheet |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: High-pressure flanges (Class 600 and up) often use spiral-wound metal gaskets, which are bought as finished parts, not cut from sheet. Module 11 covers flange classes.

## 10.5 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Test-Out

### Practice

1. **Read labels.** At the sheet rack, read the label on five rolls or sheets. Write the polymer or material, durometer, thickness, and width.
2. **Rubber by touch.** Your mentor gives you samples of red SBR, neoprene, nitrile, EPDM, and silicone with the labels hidden. Identify what you can, then check.
3. **P×T chart.** Plot 150 psi saturated steam (about 366°F) on a compressed fiber sheet P×T chart and on a graphite chart. Say which zone each falls in.
4. **Pick a material.** For each service in the 10.4 table, find a sheet item your branch stocks that fits.
5. **Counter call.** A practice customer needs a gasket for a 2 in line carrying 30% caustic soda at 120°F. Say what you would recommend and what you would confirm first.

### Knowledge check (practice, not graded)

1. What does durometer measure?
2. Which elastomer is the usual choice for oil and fuel?
3. Which elastomer is the usual choice for hot water and steam at low pressure?
4. Why is most PTFE gasket sheet filled?
5. Which filled PTFE fits strong caustics?
6. When would you choose ePTFE?
7. What is flexible graphite poor with?
8. A customer brings in an old steam gasket and asks you to cut a copy from it. What should you watch for?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your mentor.
