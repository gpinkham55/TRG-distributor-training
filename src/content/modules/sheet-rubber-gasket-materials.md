Gaskets, pads, and seals are cut from sheet and roll materials. The material decides what the gasket can seal: which media, how hot, and how much pressure. This module covers elastomer sheet rubber, compressed fiber sheet, PTFE, ePTFE, and flexible graphite, the specs that describe them, and how to pick one.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Describe the common elastomer sheet rubbers and where each fits.
2. Read a sheet rubber spec: polymer, durometer, thickness, width, cloth insertion, and grade.
3. Read the basic parts of an ASTM D2000 callout and an ASTM F104 callout.
4. Explain where compressed fiber sheet, filled PTFE, ePTFE, and flexible graphite fit, and read a P×T chart.
5. Pick a gasket material from the media, temperature, pressure, and flange.
6. Sell sheet in P21 in the right unit (SI, SF, or the roll) and store and handle it correctly.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Old gaskets, especially from steam lines and older plants, may contain asbestos. Never cut, grind, or sand an old gasket a customer brings in. Handle it as little as possible and ask your Manager how your branch deals with it.

## 10.1 Elastomer Sheet Rubber

Elastomer sheet is solid rubber in rolls or sheets. It seals well at low pressure and moderate temperature, and it is used for flange gaskets, pads, seals, and wear strips.

### Reading a sheet rubber spec

| Spec | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Polymer | The base rubber | Neoprene |
| Durometer | Hardness on the Shore A scale; higher is harder | 60 Shore A, a common gasket hardness |
| Thickness | Usually in fractions of an inch | 1/16 in, 1/8 in, 1/4 in |
| Width and form | Roll width or sheet size | 36 in or 48 in wide rolls |
| Cloth insertion | Fabric layers inside the rubber for strength | 1-ply or 2-ply insertion |
| Grade | Commercial, specification (ASTM D2000), or FDA | FDA white nitrile for food |

### Common elastomers

Temperature ranges are approximate. Check the maker's data sheet for the exact grade.

| Elastomer | Good with | Poor with | Approx. range (°F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural rubber and red SBR | Water, air, abrasion, general use | Oils, ozone, heat | -20 to 170 |
| Neoprene (CR) | Moderate oil, weather, ozone, refrigerants | Strong acids, ketones | -40 to 220 |
| Nitrile (Buna-N, NBR) | Oils, fuels, hydraulic fluid | Ozone and sunlight, ketones | -20 to 225 |
| EPDM | Water, steam, weather, many dilute acids and caustics | Petroleum oils and fuels | -40 to 300 |
| FKM (often sold as Viton) | Fuels, oils, many acids, high heat | Ketones, steam, some amines | -10 to 400 |
| Silicone | Very wide temperature range, food contact in FDA-compliant grades | Abrasion, tearing, steam at pressure | -80 to 450 |
| Gum rubber | Abrasion, cushioning; pure natural rubber | Oils, heat | -20 to 160 |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Rolls of red SBR, black neoprene, white FDA nitrile, and orange silicone sheet on a rack]

### Durometer

| Shore A | Feels like | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 40 | A pencil eraser | Soft pads, uneven surfaces |
| 60 | A tire tread | Most flange gaskets |
| 70 | A shoe heel | Gaskets with higher bolt load, wear pads |
| 80 to 90 | Hard rubber | Wear strips, high-load pads |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Sheet rubber often goes by color: "red rubber," "black neoprene," "white food grade." Read the polymer and durometer on the roll label before you cut; the color only narrows it down.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Rubber gaskets fit low pressure and moderate heat. For steam, high heat, or high pressure, step up to compressed fiber, PTFE, or graphite.

## 10.2 ASTM D2000 and F104 Callouts

Engineering drawings and customer specs often describe the material with a standard callout. Match the callout to the maker's data sheet before you cut.

### ASTM D2000 (rubber)

A D2000 callout describes a rubber compound by its heat resistance, oil swell, hardness, and strength. Example: **M2BG714**.

| Part | Meaning in the example |
|---|---|
| M | Metric (SI) units |
| 2 | Grade number: the level of extra requirements |
| B | Type: heat resistance, tested at 100°C (212°F) |
| G | Class: oil swell, 40% maximum in test oil |
| 7 | Durometer: 70 Shore A, ±5 |
| 14 | Tensile strength: 14 MPa minimum |

M2BG714 describes a typical nitrile compound. Letters and numbers after it (A14, B14, EO14) add specific tests. The 14 in a suffix is a test code. When a drawing calls out D2000, the material must meet every part of the line, so confirm it on the data sheet.

| Type letter | Heat test |
|---|---|
| A | 70°C |
| B | 100°C |
| C | 125°C |
| D | 150°C |
| E | 175°C |
| F | 200°C |
| G | 225°C |
| H | 250°C |

| Class letter | Max oil swell |
|---|---|
| A | No requirement |
| B | 140% |
| C | 120% |
| D | 100% |
| E | 80% |
| F | 60% |
| G | 40% |
| H | 30% |
| J | 20% |
| K | 10% |

### ASTM F104 (nonmetallic gasket materials)

ASTM F104 classifies fiber and other nonmetallic gasket sheet with a line callout: a type, a class, and numbers for properties such as compressibility, recovery, and fluid resistance. The maker's data sheet lists the F104 callout for each grade. Match it to the callout on the customer's drawing.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A callout on a drawing or PO is part of the order. Cut from sheet that meets it, or bring in your Manager before you quote a substitute.

## 10.3 Compressed Fiber Sheet and the P×T Chart

Compressed fiber sheet is fibers (aramid, glass, carbon, or mineral) bound with a rubber binder such as nitrile. It is the everyday gasket material for plant piping: water, low-pressure steam, oils, air, and many mild chemicals.

| Feature | What to know |
|---|---|
| Binder | Nitrile binder for oils and general service; other binders for other media |
| Thickness | 1/16 in for flat, smooth flanges; 1/8 in for older or uneven flanges |
| Strength | Much higher pressure and temperature limits than rubber sheet |
| Limits | Strong acids and caustics attack many grades; check the maker's chart |

### The P×T chart

Gasket makers rate fiber, PTFE, and graphite sheet with a **P×T chart**: a graph of pressure against temperature, split into zones.

1. Get the service pressure and temperature.
2. Plot the point on the maker's chart for that grade and thickness.
3. Read the zone.

| Zone | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Suitable under normal installation |
| 2 | Needs an engineering check with the maker |
| 3 | Unsuitable; pick another material |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A gasket maker's P×T chart with three zones shaded and one service point plotted]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Thinner is usually better. A 1/16 in fiber gasket holds more pressure and creeps less than a 1/8 in gasket of the same material, on flat, smooth flanges.

## 10.4 PTFE, ePTFE, and Flexible Graphite

### PTFE

PTFE resists almost every chemical. Plain (virgin) PTFE creeps under bolt load, so most PTFE gasket sheet is **filled** to resist creep.

| Type | What it is | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Filled PTFE (silica) | PTFE with silica filler | Strong acids |
| Filled PTFE (barium sulfate) | PTFE with barium sulfate filler | Strong caustics and many acids |
| Filled PTFE (glass microspheres) | PTFE with hollow glass spheres; more compressible | Lower bolt loads, plastic and lined flanges |
| ePTFE (expanded) | Soft, stretched PTFE that conforms to the flange | Uneven, damaged, glass-lined, or FRP flanges |
| PTFE envelope | A PTFE jacket over a rubber or fiber insert | Chemical service where only the PTFE touches the media |

### Flexible graphite

Flexible graphite handles very high temperatures and is a standard choice for steam and hot oil. It often has a thin stainless steel insert for strength and handling.

- Good with steam, hot water, heat transfer oils, and most chemicals.
- Poor with strong oxidizers such as concentrated nitric acid.
- The surface marks easily. Handle it by the edges and keep it flat.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Sheets of filled PTFE, ePTFE, and flexible graphite with a tanged stainless insert, side by side]

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Graphite and some PTFE grades lose their seal when creased. Store them flat and handle them by the edges.

## 10.5 Choosing a Gasket Material

Start with the media, then check temperature and pressure, then the flange.

| Service | First choices | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Cold water, air, low pressure | Red SBR, neoprene, EPDM, compressed fiber | Nothing special |
| Hot water and low-pressure steam | EPDM (lower pressure), compressed fiber | Nitrile, neoprene, natural rubber |
| Higher-pressure steam | Flexible graphite, high-temperature compressed fiber | Rubber sheet |
| Oils, fuels, hydraulic fluid | Nitrile, FKM, nitrile-bound compressed fiber | EPDM, natural rubber |
| Strong acids | Silica-filled PTFE, ePTFE, FKM for some acids | Most rubber, most fiber sheet |
| Strong caustics | Barium sulfate-filled PTFE, EPDM at lower temperatures | Silica-filled PTFE |
| Food and beverage | FDA-compliant white nitrile, EPDM, silicone, or PTFE | Anything unmarked for food |
| Glass-lined, plastic, or FRP flanges | ePTFE, soft rubber | Hard sheet that needs high bolt load |

Only sheet the maker's label or data sheet states is FDA-compliant goes in food service.

### Steps: pick a gasket material

1. Get the exact media, concentration, temperature, and pressure.
2. Pick the families that handle the media from the table above.
3. Check the maker's chemical chart for the grade.
4. Plot pressure and temperature on the maker's P×T chart.
5. Check the flange: material, condition, and class (Module 11).
6. Pick the thickness: 1/16 in for flat, smooth flanges; thicker for uneven ones.
7. When the service is outside what you know, stop and ask your Manager.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: For steam, chemicals, and anything above low pressure, confirm the material with the maker's chemical chart and P×T rating.

### Industry applications

| Industry | Common gasket jobs | Usual materials |
|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Steam headers, digester flanges, bleach plant piping, paper machine seals | Graphite, compressed fiber, ePTFE, EPDM |
| Agriculture | Pump and tank lid gaskets, irrigation flanges, equipment covers | EPDM, nitrile, neoprene, fiber sheet |
| Chemical | Acid and caustic lines, lined vessels, tank manways | Filled PTFE, ePTFE, FKM, EPDM |
| Heavy equipment | Hydraulic tank and manifold gaskets, covers, pads | Nitrile, neoprene, fiber sheet |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: High-pressure flanges (Class 600 and up) often use spiral-wound metal gaskets, bought as finished parts. Module 11 covers flange classes.

## 10.6 Sheet in P21, Storage, and Handling

### Selling sheet in P21

| The customer wants | Enter |
|---|---|
| A cut piece, 12 in × 24 in | 288 SI of the sheet item |
| A larger piece, 3 ft × 4 ft | 12 SF of the sheet item, or 1,728 SI when the item sells in SI |
| A full roll | The roll item, or the roll's full SF; confirm the roll size on the label |
| A cut gasket | The gasket item in EA, or a quote from Module 12 |

1. Press **F2** and search by material, thickness, and durometer ("neoprene 1/8 60").
2. Read the **UOM** before you enter a quantity.
3. Check **net available**. On sheet, P21 counts total SI or SF; one roll may be short of the piece the customer needs. Have the warehouse check the roll.
4. Put the size and any cut instructions in the line's extended description.

### Storage and handling

- Store rubber rolls horizontal on a rack or bar, out of sunlight, away from motors and welders (ozone).
- Store graphite, PTFE, and fiber sheet flat.
- Keep FDA sheet bagged and away from oily stock.
- Label every partial roll and cut piece with the material, thickness, and durometer.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never cut a gasket from unlabeled sheet or a remnant you can't identify. A wrong material in steam or chemical service could be a catastrophic failure.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Read the label every time: material, thickness, durometer. The roll color only narrows it down.

## 10.7 Case Study, What If, and Rookie Mistakes

### Case study: the bleach plant gasket

A practice caller, a planner at Southeastern Paper Mill, calls: "We keep losing the gaskets on the caustic line in the bleach plant. Same red rubber every time. Can you send something better?"

1. **Gather the gasket spec: flange, media, temperature, pressure.** 4 in Class 150 raised face flanges, carbon steel, in good shape. Media: 50% caustic soda. Temperature: 160°F. Pressure: 90 psi.
2. **The failure.** Red SBR swells and softens in strong caustic at that temperature. That explains the repeat leaks.
3. **Chemical chart.** The maker rates barium sulfate-filled PTFE excellent with 50% caustic at 160°F, and silica-filled PTFE poor. EPDM rates good at lower temperatures and fair at 160°F.
4. **P×T chart.** 90 psi at 160°F plots in zone 1 for 1/16 in barium sulfate-filled PTFE.
5. **P21.** F2 shows 1/16 in barium sulfate-filled PTFE sheet in SI. The rep quotes cut ring gaskets for the 4 in Class 150 flange (Module 12) and writes "50% caustic, 160°F, replaces red SBR" in the extended description.
6. **Follow-up.** The rep tells the planner to replace every gasket on that line at the next shutdown.

The flange was fine. The material was wrong for the media from the start.

### What if...

| Situation | What to do | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A customer wants "red rubber" for steam | Check the steam pressure and temperature; offer EPDM for low-pressure steam inside its temperature limit, and fiber or graphite above that | Red rubber, neoprene, and nitrile fail on steam |
| A drawing calls out ASTM D2000 | Match the full callout on a data sheet before you cut | The callout is part of the order |
| A customer brings an old steam gasket to copy | Don't cut, grind, or sand it; ask your Manager | It may contain asbestos |
| The roll label is missing | Set the roll aside until it is identified | A wrong material fails in service |
| A chemical is missing from the chart | Stop and ask your Manager or the maker | Compatibility can't be guessed |
| Glass-lined or FRP flanges | Offer ePTFE or soft rubber | Hard sheet needs bolt load those flanges can't take |

### Rookie mistakes

| Mistake | What it causes | The habit that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Picking sheet by color | The wrong polymer in service | Read the label |
| Red rubber, neoprene, or nitrile on steam | A blown gasket and a burn hazard | Use EPDM for low-pressure steam inside its temperature limit. Use fiber or graphite above that |
| Silica-filled PTFE on caustic | A failed gasket | Use barium sulfate-filled PTFE on caustic |
| 1/8 in fiber on flat, smooth flanges | More creep and lower pressure capacity | Use 1/16 in when the flanges allow |
| Folding graphite sheet | A creased gasket that leaks | Store flat, handle by the edges |
| Entering SF when the item sells in SI | A wrong quantity and price | Read the UOM |

## 10.8 Gasket Material Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Elastomer | A rubber material |
| Durometer | Hardness on the Shore A scale |
| Cloth insertion | Fabric plies inside rubber sheet |
| ASTM D2000 | A callout system for rubber compounds |
| ASTM F104 | A classification system for nonmetallic gasket materials |
| Compressed fiber sheet | Fibers bound with a rubber binder |
| Binder | The rubber that holds fiber sheet together |
| P×T chart | The maker's pressure and temperature rating chart |
| Creep | Slow flow of a gasket under bolt load |
| Filled PTFE | PTFE with filler to resist creep |
| ePTFE | Expanded PTFE; soft and conforming |
| PTFE envelope | A PTFE jacket over a gasket insert |
| Flexible graphite | Graphite sheet for high heat and steam |
| Tanged insert | A thin stainless sheet with punched teeth inside graphite gaskets |
| FDA grade | Sheet the maker's label or data sheet states is FDA-compliant for food contact |
| Ozone | A gas from motors and welders that cracks some rubbers |

## 10.9 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Module Quiz

### Practice

1. **Read labels.** At the sheet rack, read the label on five rolls or sheets. Write the material, durometer, thickness, and width.
2. **Rubber by touch.** Your Manager gives you samples of red SBR, neoprene, nitrile, EPDM, and silicone with the labels hidden. Identify what you can, then check.
3. **D2000.** Your Manager gives you a D2000 callout from a drawing. Break it into its parts and find a sheet that meets it.
4. **P×T chart.** Plot 150 psi saturated steam (about 366°F) on a compressed fiber chart and a graphite chart. Say which zone each falls in.
5. **Pick a material.** For each service in the 10.5 table, find a sheet item your branch stocks that fits.
6. **P21.** Press F2 and find 1/8 in 60 durometer neoprene. Enter a 12 in × 36 in piece in its UOM.

### Knowledge check (practice)

1. What does durometer measure?
2. In M2BG714, what do the 7 and the 14 mean?
3. What is the usual lowest-cost choice for oil and fuel at moderate heat?
4. Which elastomer is the usual choice for hot water and steam at low pressure?
5. Why is most PTFE gasket sheet filled?
6. Which filled PTFE fits strong caustics?
7. When would you choose ePTFE?
8. A point plots in zone 3 on a P×T chart. What do you do?
9. A customer brings in an old steam gasket and asks you to cut a copy. What do you watch for?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your Manager.
