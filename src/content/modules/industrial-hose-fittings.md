Industrial hose moves water, air, steam, chemicals, and bulk material in every plant, farm, and job site. This module covers the main hose families, the couplings that go on them, and how couplings are held on the hose. It builds on the STAMPED method and the ratings in Module 4. Module 7 covers hydraulic hose; Module 8 covers pneumatic tubing; Module 9 covers building assemblies as kits.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Match a customer's job to the right family of industrial hose.
2. Pick hose for the suction side and the discharge side of a pump.
3. Identify cam and groove, ground joint, universal, threaded, and flanged couplings by sight.
4. Read cam and groove type letters (A, B, C, D, E, F, DC, DP) and pick the coupling material and gasket for the media.
5. Pick an attachment method and clamp that fit the hose, the media, and the pressure.
6. Follow the steam hose rules for couplings, clamps, and retightening.
7. Find industrial hose and couplings in P21 and record a complete assembly spec.
8. Give examples of industrial hose in pulp and paper, agriculture, chemical, and heavy equipment work.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A coupling that blows off a pressurized hose whips and injures people. Match the coupling, the attachment method, and the clamp to the hose and the working pressure. On steam, use only the couplings and clamps the hose maker approves.

## 5.1 Industrial Hose Families

Industrial hose falls into a few families, each built for one kind of job. Start with the family, then use STAMPED to pick the exact hose.

| Family | What it carries | How it is built | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water discharge and washdown | Water, hot water, mild cleaners | Rubber or PVC tube, textile reinforcement; washdown hose has a tough cover for dragging | Hot water ratings; washdown hose often sees steam during cleanup |
| Air and multipurpose | Compressed air, water, light oils | Rubber or PVC, textile reinforcement | Jackhammer and air drill hose carries a higher rating (often 300 psi or more) than general air hose |
| Suction and discharge | Water, slurry, fuel, chemicals pumped in and out | A helix holds the hose open under vacuum | Check the vacuum rating |
| Layflat | Water and light liquids on the discharge side | Woven jacket with a rubber, PVC, or polyurethane lining; rolls flat when empty | Discharge only |
| Steam | Saturated steam for cleaning, heating, process | EPDM tube, wire braid, heat-resistant cover | Steam-rated couplings and clamps only (5.3) |
| Chemical transfer | Acids, caustics, solvents | UHMWPE, cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE), or EPDM tube; often a static wire | Check compatibility for every chemical (Module 4) |
| PTFE-lined | Aggressive or hot chemicals, sticky products | PTFE liner inside stainless braid or rubber; smooth bore or convoluted | Fittings are built on at the factory or with special equipment |
| Material handling | Sand, cement, grain, pellets, slurry | Thick natural rubber tube for abrasion; static wire for dry bulk | Wear at bends; grounding for dry powder |
| Food grade | Milk, juice, beverages, edible oils | White tube made from food-contact compounds | Must be marked food grade for the product |

### Suction or discharge?

A pump has two sides. The **suction** side pulls the media in and puts the hose under vacuum. The **discharge** side pushes the media out under pressure.

| Side | Hose that fits |
|---|---|
| Suction (pump inlet) | Hardwall suction hose with a helix, rated for the vacuum |
| Discharge (pump outlet) | Discharge hose, layflat, or suction and discharge hose rated for the pressure |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: When a customer says "pump hose," ask which side of the pump. A discharge hose on the suction side collapses and starves the pump.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Clear PVC suction hose lets the operator see the flow, which helps on farms and job sites. It loses strength fast in heat and sunlight. For hot media or heavy daily use, step up to rubber.

### Reading an industrial layline

An industrial layline usually shows the maker, the product line, the ID, the working pressure, and often the service: "STEAM 250 PSI," "CHEMICAL," "FOOD." Some add a vacuum rating and a date code. Write every word on the layline into your notes before you quote.

## 5.2 Couplings

A coupling joins the hose to a pump, a tank, a valve, or another hose. Five coupling styles cover most industrial jobs.

### Cam and groove

Cam and groove couplings connect and disconnect by hand. The female half (the **coupler**) has two cam arms. The male half (the **adapter**) has a groove. Close the arms and the cams pull the adapter into a gasket in the coupler. Cam and groove dimensions follow a common standard (A-A-59326), so one maker's half connects to another maker's half of the same size.

| Type | Half | Other end |
|---|---|---|
| A | Adapter (male groove) | Female NPT thread |
| B | Coupler (female, cam arms) | Male NPT thread |
| C | Coupler (female, cam arms) | Hose shank |
| D | Coupler (female, cam arms) | Female NPT thread |
| E | Adapter (male groove) | Hose shank |
| F | Adapter (male groove) | Male NPT thread |
| DC | Dust cap: closes an adapter | None |
| DP | Dust plug: closes a coupler | None |

A common hose has a type C on one end and a type E on the other, so lengths connect end to end.

| Material | Where it fits |
|---|---|
| Aluminum | Light, general water and fuel service |
| Polypropylene | Light; many chemicals and fertilizers; lower pressure and heat |
| Brass | Water, fuel, petroleum |
| Stainless steel | Chemicals, food, caustic, high wear |

The **gasket** inside the coupler is usually nitrile (Buna-N). EPDM, FKM, and PTFE gaskets fit other media. Check the gasket against the media on the compatibility chart, the same as the hose.

Cam and groove pressure ratings drop as size goes up, and they vary by material. Read the maker's chart for the size and material before you quote a pressure.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Relieve the pressure and drain the line before anyone opens cam arms. Some couplers have locking arms that release first.

### Ground joint couplings

A ground joint coupling seals metal to metal with a machined ball and seat, held together by a wing nut or a threaded nut. The hose end is held with an **interlocking clamp** that grips a shoulder on the stem. Ground joint couplings are the standard for **steam hose** and common on high-pressure air and water.

### Universal (Chicago) couplings

Universal couplings join with a quarter turn of two lugs. The lugs are the same on every size, so any size connects to any other. They are common on air compressors, jackhammers, and air tools.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Universal couplings vibrate loose. Put safety pins through the lugs and a whip check (a cable restraint across the joint) on every air hose connection. A loose air hose whips with enough force to injure someone.

### Threaded fittings

| Fitting | Use |
|---|---|
| King nipple (hose shank by male NPT) | General water, air, and suction hose with a clamp |
| Combination nipple | Water and suction hose, held with a double-bolt clamp |
| Hose barb | Light-duty hose with a worm gear clamp |
| GHT (garden hose thread) | Washdown and light water hose |
| NST (National Standard Thread) | Fire hose and some municipal water equipment |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: NPT and GHT look alike in 3/4 in size, and they are different threads. GHT seals on a washer; NPT seals on the threads. When a customer says a fitting "won't seal," check for two thread types forced together.

### Flanged ends

Large suction and discharge hose, chemical hose, and expansion joints often end in a flange: a fixed or floating flange that bolts to a pipe flange with a gasket between. Flanged ends follow the ASME B16.5 bolt pattern for the size and pressure class (Module 11).

| Coupling | Connects by | Typical service |
|---|---|---|
| Cam and groove | Cam arms, by hand | Transfer, pumping, unloading |
| Ground joint | Ball and seat, wing nut | Steam, high-pressure air and water |
| Universal | Quarter turn of two lugs | Compressed air, air tools |
| Threaded | NPT, GHT, or NST thread | Water, air, general service |
| Flanged | Bolts and a gasket | Large hose, chemical lines, pipe connections |

## 5.3 Attachment Methods and Clamps

A coupling holds only as well as the way it is attached. Pick the method from the pressure, the media, and the hose maker's coupling chart.

| Method | How it works | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Worm gear clamp | A band tightened by a screw | Light-duty, low-pressure water and air |
| T-bolt clamp | A heavy band with a bolt | Larger suction and discharge hose |
| Double-bolt clamp | Two bolted halves around the hose | Combination nipples on water and suction hose |
| Interlocking clamp | Bolted halves that grip the hose and a shoulder on the stem | Ground joint couplings on steam and high-pressure air |
| Banding | Steel band pulled tight and locked with a tool | Water, air, and chemical hose; fast to build in the shop |
| Crimped ferrule | A metal sleeve squeezed onto the hose by a crimper | Permanent fittings with high holding power; many industrial and all hydraulic hose |
| Built-in or swaged ends | Fittings attached at the factory or with special tools | PTFE-lined and some chemical hose |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Four hose ends side by side: a worm gear clamp, a double-bolt clamp, an interlocking clamp on a ground joint coupling, and a crimped ferrule]

### Picking the method

| If the job is... | Use |
|---|---|
| Steam | Ground joint couplings with interlocking clamps, or crimped steam fittings the hose maker approves |
| High-pressure air (jackhammer, air drill) | Crimped fittings or interlocking clamps; safety pins and whip checks on universal couplings |
| Suction and discharge, 2 in and up | Crimped ferrules, banding, or T-bolt or double-bolt clamps per the maker's chart |
| Chemical transfer | Crimped ferrules or banding with stainless fittings; factory ends on PTFE-lined hose |
| Light water or air at low pressure | Worm gear clamp on a barb or king nipple |

### Steam hose rules

1. Use steam-rated hose with steam-rated couplings: ground joint couplings with interlocking clamps, or crimped steam fittings the hose maker approves.
2. Keep worm gear clamps and banding off steam hose.
3. Bolted clamps on steam hose loosen as the hose heats and cools. Tell the customer to retighten them after the first use and check them on a schedule.
4. Tag every steam assembly with its rating and test date (Module 9).

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Steam burns are severe. A steam coupling that blows off releases scalding steam and whips the hose. Follow the hose maker's coupling chart exactly on steam.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: The hose maker's catalog lists the couplings and clamps approved for each hose. When habit and the catalog disagree, follow the catalog.

## 5.4 Industry Applications

| Industry | Job | Typical hose | Typical couplings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Washdown stations on the paper machine | Hot water washdown hose | Ground joint or crimped, with a spray nozzle |
| Pulp and paper | Steam cleaning and heating | Steam hose | Ground joint with interlocking clamps |
| Pulp and paper | Pumping pulp stock or lime slurry | Material handling or suction and discharge hose | Flanged or cam and groove |
| Pulp and paper | Caustic and bleach chemical transfer | Chemical transfer hose with static wire | Stainless cam and groove or flanged |
| Agriculture | Irrigation and ditch pumps | Suction hose on the inlet, layflat on the outlet | Cam and groove |
| Agriculture | Liquid fertilizer and spray rigs | Chemical-resistant suction and spray hose | Polypropylene or stainless cam and groove |
| Chemical | Unloading tank trucks and railcars | Chemical transfer or PTFE-lined hose with static wire | Stainless cam and groove or flanged; gaskets matched to the chemical |
| Heavy equipment | Dewatering a job site | Suction and discharge hose, layflat | Cam and groove |
| Heavy equipment | Jackhammers and air tools | Air drill hose | Universal couplings with safety pins and whip checks |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Tank truck drivers often carry their own hose and arrive with the wrong coupling for the plant. A plant that unloads many chemicals may need a hose and coupling set for each one, marked so they are never mixed up.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: The same 2 in cam and groove fitting shows up on a farm, a job site, and a chemical plant. What changes is the fitting material, the gasket, and the hose behind it.

## 5.5 Industrial Hose in P21 and at the Counter

### Finding hose and couplings

1. Press **F2** and search bulk hose by family and size ("suction ," "steam "), or by the maker's part number from the layline.
2. Search couplings by type, size, and material ("cam C 2 SS," "ground joint"). Your mentor shows you how your branch describes them.
3. Read the **UOM**: bulk hose in FT, couplings and clamps in EA.
4. Read **net available** for the hose and every coupling and clamp. One missing clamp holds up the whole assembly.
5. Check the **Substitute** tab for approved alternates with the same material and rating.

### Entering the assembly

Enter a hose assembly as a **kit item** on the order (Module 9). Put the STAMPED answers, overall length, end types, and orientation in the order line's **extended description** so the shop builds what the customer asked for.

### At the counter

| The customer brings | Do this |
|---|---|
| A failed assembly | Ask what it carried, glove up, read the layline, measure ID and overall length, and identify both ends |
| A loose fitting | Identify the style and size; on cam and groove, read the type letter and measure the groove OD |
| A thread they can't match | Measure with calipers and a thread gauge; GHT, NPT, and NST look alike |
| A request for "the same as last time" | Press F8 and check Customer Sales History for the kit item |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Treat a customer's failed hose as chemical-soaked until you know what it carried. Ask before you handle it, and wear gloves.

## 5.6 Case Study, What If, and Rookie Mistakes

### Case study: the irrigation pump

A practice caller, a farmer, comes to the counter: "My irrigation pump hose collapsed. I need new hose on both sides of the pump today."

1. **Which side.** The collapsed hose was on the suction side. It is a red layflat hose with no helix.
2. **STAMPED, suction side.** Size: 3 in ID, 20 ft. Temperature: pond water, ambient summer heat. Application: pulled from a pond, lying in the sun. Media: water with sand. Pressure: suction, about 20 inHg. Ends: 3 in cam and groove, type C on the pump end and type E on the strainer end. Delivery: today.
3. **STAMPED, discharge side.** Size: 3 in ID, 300 ft to the field. Pressure: 80 psi. Ends: 3 in cam and groove. The farmer wants it to roll up after the season.
4. **The pick.** Suction side: rubber hardwall suction hose with a helix rated for the vacuum. Discharge side: layflat rated for 80 psi plus surges.
5. **Couplings.** Aluminum cam and groove with nitrile gaskets for water. Crimped ferrules on the suction hose; banding on the layflat, per the maker's chart.
6. **P21.** F2 shows 40 FT of 3 in suction hose net available and four 100 FT rolls of 3 in layflat. The rep enters both assemblies as kit items with the spec in each line's extended description.
7. **Ready time.** The shop builds the suction assembly in an hour. The rep tells the farmer the layflat lengths are ready in four hours.

The pump starved for water: layflat was on the suction side. The right hose on each side keeps the pump fed and the water moving.

### What if...

| Situation | What to do | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A customer asks for "pump hose" | Ask which side of the pump | Suction needs a helix; discharge needs a pressure rating |
| A customer wants worm gear clamps on steam hose | Decline them and offer ground joint couplings with interlocking clamps | Worm gear clamps fail on steam |
| A cam and groove coupler leaks at the gasket | Check the gasket material against the media, then the gasket's condition | A swollen or worn gasket leaks |
| An air hose coupling keeps coming apart | Add safety pins and a whip check; check the lugs for wear | Universal couplings vibrate loose |
| A fitting "won't seal" on a 3/4 in water line | Check for GHT forced onto NPT, or the reverse | The two threads look alike and seal differently |
| A tank truck driver needs a different coupling than the plant's | Ask the plant which connection it runs and match it | The plant's connection decides the coupling |
| The coupling chart lists no approved fitting for the hose | Ask your mentor or the hose maker | An unapproved coupling may blow off |

### Rookie mistakes

| Mistake | What it causes | The habit that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Layflat or softwall hose on the suction side | A collapsed hose and a starved pump | Ask which side of the pump |
| Worm gear clamps on steam hose | A blown coupling and scalding steam | Use the maker's steam couplings and clamps |
| Mixing up type C and type E | Two hoses that won't connect | Read the type letter: C is a coupler, E is an adapter |
| Checking the hose and skipping the gasket | A leak at every connection | Check the gasket on the compatibility chart |
| Aluminum cam and groove on caustic | Pitted, failed fittings | Match the fitting material to the media |
| Quoting a cam and groove pressure from memory | An assembly over its rating | Read the maker's chart for size and material |
| Missing clamps or ferrules on the order | An assembly the shop can't finish | Check net available on every component |

## 5.7 Industrial Hose Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Suction side | The pump inlet; the hose is under vacuum |
| Discharge side | The pump outlet; the hose is under pressure |
| Hardwall | Suction-rated hose with a helix |
| Layflat | Discharge hose that rolls flat when empty |
| Static wire | A wire in the hose that carries static to ground |
| Coupler | The female half of a cam and groove coupling, with cam arms |
| Adapter | The male half of a cam and groove coupling, with a groove |
| Type C / Type E | Coupler by hose shank / adapter by hose shank |
| Dust cap / dust plug | Closes an adapter / closes a coupler |
| Ground joint | A ball-and-seat coupling sealed metal to metal; the steam standard |
| Universal (Chicago) coupling | A quarter-turn lug coupling for air |
| Whip check | A cable restraint across an air hose joint |
| King nipple | A hose shank by male NPT fitting |
| Combination nipple | A fitting held with a double-bolt clamp |
| NPT / GHT / NST | National Pipe Thread / garden hose thread / National Standard Thread (fire hose) |
| Interlocking clamp | A bolted clamp that grips the hose and a shoulder on the stem |
| Banding | A steel band locked around the hose with a tool |
| Ferrule | A metal sleeve crimped over the hose end |
| Kit item | How a hose assembly is entered on an order (Module 9) |
