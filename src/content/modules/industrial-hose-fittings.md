Industrial hose moves water, air, steam, chemicals, and bulk material in every plant, farm, and job site. This module covers the main hose families, the couplings that go on them, and how couplings are attached. It builds on the STAMPED method and the ratings in Module 4.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Match a customer's job to the right family of industrial hose.
2. Identify cam and groove, ground joint, universal, and threaded couplings by sight.
3. Read cam and groove type letters (A, B, C, D, E, F, DC, DP).
4. Choose an attachment method and clamp that fit the hose and the pressure.
5. Give examples of industrial hose in each of the four industries.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A coupling that blows off a pressurized hose can whip and injure people. Match the coupling, the attachment method, and the clamp to the hose and the working pressure. Never mix and match on steam.

## 5.1 Industrial Hose Types

Industrial hose falls into a few families. Each family is built for one kind of job. Start with the family, then use STAMPED to pick the exact hose.

| Family | What it carries | How it is built | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water discharge and washdown | Water, hot water, mild cleaners | Rubber or PVC tube, textile reinforcement; washdown hose has a tough cover for dragging | Hot water ratings; washdown hose often sees steam during cleanup |
| Air and multipurpose | Compressed air, water, light oils | Rubber or PVC, textile reinforcement | Jackhammer and air drill hose is rated higher (often 300 psi or more) than general air hose |
| Suction and discharge | Water, slurry, fuel, chemicals pumped in and out | A helix holds the hose open under vacuum | Rated for vacuum; layflat cannot be used on suction |
| Layflat | Water and light liquids on the discharge side | Woven jacket with a rubber, PVC, or polyurethane lining; rolls flat when empty | Discharge only; no vacuum rating |
| Steam | Saturated steam for cleaning, heating, process | EPDM tube, wire braid, heat-resistant cover | Steam-rated couplings and clamps only (5.3) |
| Chemical transfer | Acids, caustics, solvents | UHMWPE, cross-linked polyethylene (XLPE), or EPDM tube; often a static wire | Check compatibility for every chemical (Module 4) |
| PTFE-lined | Aggressive or hot chemicals, sticky products | PTFE liner inside stainless braid or rubber; smooth bore or convoluted | Fittings are usually built on at the factory or with special equipment |
| Material handling | Sand, cement, grain, pellets, slurry | Thick natural rubber tube for abrasion; static wire for dry bulk | Wear at bends; grounding for dry powder |
| Food grade | Milk, juice, beverages, edible oils | White tube made from food-contact compounds | Must be marked food grade for the product |

### Suction or discharge?

A pump has two sides. The **suction** side pulls the media in and puts the hose under vacuum. The **discharge** side pushes the media out under pressure.

| Side | Hose needed |
|---|---|
| Suction (inlet of the pump) | Suction hose with a helix, rated for vacuum |
| Discharge (outlet of the pump) | Discharge hose, layflat, or suction and discharge hose rated for the pressure |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: When a customer says "pump hose," ask which side of the pump. A discharge hose on the suction side collapses and starves the pump.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Clear PVC suction hose lets the operator see the flow, which helps on farms and job sites. It loses strength fast in heat and sunlight. For hot media or heavy daily use, step up to rubber.

## 5.2 Couplings

A coupling joins the hose to a pump, a tank, a valve, or another hose. Four coupling styles cover most industrial jobs.

### Cam and groove

Cam and groove couplings connect and disconnect by hand, with no tools. The female half (the **coupler**) has two cam arms. The male half (the **adapter**) has a groove. Close the arms and the cams pull the adapter into a gasket in the coupler.

Each style has a letter. Learn these; customers and catalogs use them.

| Type | Half | Other end |
|---|---|---|
| A | Adapter (male groove) | Female NPT thread |
| B | Coupler (female, cam arms) | Male NPT thread |
| C | Coupler (female, cam arms) | Hose shank |
| D | Coupler (female, cam arms) | Female NPT thread |
| E | Adapter (male groove) | Hose shank |
| F | Adapter (male groove) | Male NPT thread |
| DC | Dust cap: closes an adapter | None |
| DP | Dust plug: closes a coupler | None |

A common hose has a type C on one end and a type E on the other, so lengths connect end to end.

**Materials:** aluminum (light, general use), polypropylene (light, many chemicals), brass, and stainless steel (chemicals, food, high wear). The **gasket** inside the coupler is usually nitrile (Buna-N); EPDM, FKM, and PTFE gaskets are available for other media. Check the gasket for compatibility too.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never open cam arms on a line under pressure. Some couplers have locking arms that must be released first. Relieve the pressure and drain the line before you disconnect.

### Ground joint (boss) couplings

A ground joint coupling has a machined ball and seat that seal metal to metal, held together by a wing nut or a threaded nut. The hose end is clamped with an **interlocking clamp** that grips a shoulder on the stem. Ground joint couplings are the standard for **steam hose** and are common on high-pressure air and water.

### Universal (Chicago) couplings

Universal couplings join with a quarter turn of two lugs. Every size of universal coupling fits every other size. They are common on air compressors, jackhammers, and air tools.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Universal couplings can vibrate loose. Use safety pins through the lugs and a whip check (a cable restraint across the joint) on air hose. A loose air hose can whip with enough force to injure someone.

### Threaded fittings

| Fitting | Use |
|---|---|
| King nipple (hose shank by male NPT) | General water, air, and suction hose with a clamp |
| Combination nipple | Water and suction hose, held with a double-bolt clamp |
| Hose barb | Light-duty hose with a worm gear clamp |
| GHT (garden hose thread) | Washdown and light water hose |
| NST (National Standard Thread) | Fire hose and some municipal water equipment |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: NPT and GHT can look alike in 3/4 in size but do not fit together. GHT seals on a washer, not on the threads. When a customer says a fitting "won't seal," check whether two thread types were forced together.

## 5.3 Attachment Methods and Clamps

The coupling is only as good as the way it is held on the hose. Pick the attachment method from the pressure, the media, and the hose maker's chart.

| Method | How it works | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Worm gear clamp | A band tightened by a screw | Light-duty, low-pressure hose; not for steam or high-pressure air |
| T-bolt clamp | A heavy band with a bolt | Larger suction and discharge hose |
| Double-bolt clamp | Two bolted halves around the hose | Combination nipples on water and suction hose |
| Interlocking clamp | Bolted halves that grip both the hose and a shoulder on the stem | Ground joint couplings on steam and high-pressure air |
| Banding | Steel band pulled tight and locked with a tool | Water, air, and chemical hose; fast to build in the shop |
| Crimped ferrule | A metal sleeve squeezed onto the hose by a crimper | Permanent fittings with high holding power; many industrial and all hydraulic hose |
| Built-in or swaged ends | Fittings attached at the factory or with special tools | PTFE-lined and some chemical hose |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Four hose ends side by side: a worm gear clamp, a double-bolt clamp, an interlocking clamp on a ground joint coupling, and a crimped ferrule]

### Steam hose rules

1. Use steam-rated hose with steam-rated couplings: ground joint couplings with interlocking clamps, or crimped steam fittings approved by the hose maker.
2. Never use worm gear clamps or banding on steam hose.
3. Bolted clamps on steam hose loosen as the hose heats and cools. Tell the customer to retighten them after the first use and check them on a schedule.
4. Tag every steam assembly with its rating and test date (Module 9).

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Steam burns are severe. A steam coupling that blows off releases scalding steam and whips the hose. Follow the hose maker's coupling chart exactly for steam.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: The hose maker's catalog lists the couplings and clamps approved for each hose. When in doubt, the catalog wins over habit.

## 5.4 Industry Applications

| Industry | Job | Typical hose | Typical couplings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Washdown stations on the paper machine | Hot water washdown hose | Ground joint or crimped, with a spray nozzle |
| Pulp and paper | Steam cleaning and heating | Steam hose | Ground joint with interlocking clamps |
| Pulp and paper | Pumping pulp stock or lime slurry | Material handling or suction and discharge hose | Flanged or cam and groove |
| Agriculture | Irrigation pumps and ditch pumps | Suction hose on the inlet, layflat on the outlet | Cam and groove |
| Agriculture | Liquid fertilizer and spray rigs | Chemical-resistant suction and spray hose | Polypropylene or stainless cam and groove |
| Chemical | Unloading tank trucks and railcars | Chemical transfer or PTFE-lined hose with static wire | Stainless cam and groove or flanged; gaskets matched to the chemical |
| Heavy equipment | Dewatering a job site | Suction and discharge hose, layflat | Cam and groove |
| Heavy equipment | Jackhammers and air tools | Air drill hose | Universal couplings with safety pins and whip checks |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Tank truck drivers often carry their own hose but arrive with the wrong coupling for the plant. A plant that unloads many chemicals may need a hose and coupling set for each one, marked so they are never mixed up.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: The same 2 in cam and groove fitting shows up on a farm, a job site, and a chemical plant. What changes is the material of the fitting, the gasket, and the hose behind it.
