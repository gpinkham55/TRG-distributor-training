## 16.1 Rubber Expansion Joints

A rubber expansion joint is a flexible section bolted into a pipeline. It absorbs movement from heat, cuts vibration and noise from pumps, and eases small misalignment.

| Type | Where it fits |
|---|---|
| Single arch | General piping; pump suction and discharge |
| Multiple arch | Larger movements |
| Filled arch | Slurry and abrasive media; the arch is filled so solids don't pack into it, with less movement |
| Reducer (tapered) | Joins two pipe sizes |

### Specs to gather

| Spec | Example |
|---|---|
| Pipe size | 6 in |
| Face-to-face length | 6 in |
| Flange drilling | Class 150 (Module 11) |
| Media, temperature, pressure, and vacuum | Water at 180°F, 100 psi, full vacuum on the suction side |
| Movements | Axial compression and extension, lateral, angular |
| Elastomer | EPDM for hot water; nitrile for oil (Module 10) |
| Control rods | Yes or no |

**Control rods** are steel rods across the joint that stop it from stretching too far when the pipe isn't anchored well enough to hold the pressure thrust. The customer's engineer decides whether they are needed.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A single-arch rubber expansion joint with control rods installed between a pump and its discharge pipe]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: An expansion joint is not a fix for badly misaligned pipe. A joint forced into a misaligned gap can fail and release the media. If the customer's pipe is out of line beyond the joint's rated movement, the pipe needs fixing first.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Mills and chemical plants often replace expansion joints during outages from a list of tag numbers. Keep the face-to-face length, drilling, and elastomer on file for each tag, and the next quote takes minutes.

## 16.2 Conveyor and Drive Belting

### Conveyor belting

| Spec | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Belt width in inches | 24 in |
| Plies and rating | Fabric layers and the strength in pounds per inch of width (PIW) | 2-ply, 220 PIW |
| Cover thickness | Top cover × bottom cover | 3/16 in × 1/16 in |
| Cover grade | Abrasion, heat, oil, or food resistance | Abrasion-resistant for gravel; heat-resistant for hot ash |
| Splice | How the ends join | Mechanical fasteners (fast) or vulcanized (stronger, smoother) |

### Drive belts

V-belts carry power from a motor to a pump, fan, or machine. The part number gives the section and the length.

| Belt type | Sections | How to read the number |
|---|---|---|
| Classical | A, B, C, D | A48 = A section, about 48 in inside length |
| Narrow (wedge) | 3V, 5V, 8V | 5V1000 = 5V section, 100.0 in effective length |
| Light duty (FHP) | 2L, 3L, 4L, 5L | 4L480 = 4L section, 48.0 in outside length |
| Banded | Two or more belts joined across the top | 3/5V1000 = three 5V belts banded together |

- On a drive with more than one groove, replace all the belts as a **matched set**.
- Agricultural equipment often uses belts with the equipment maker's own part numbers. Cross-reference the number before you quote.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A section of conveyor belt showing the plies and covers, beside classical and narrow V-belts with their part numbers visible]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Belts and pulleys can pull in hands and clothing. Tell customers to lock out the machine before measuring or changing a belt.

## 16.3 O-Rings, Packing, and Mechanical Seals

### O-rings

An O-ring is sized by its **inside diameter (ID)** and **cross section (CS)**. Most inch O-rings follow the AS568 standard, which gives each size a dash number. The first digit of the dash number tells you the cross section.

| AS568 series | Cross section (in) |
|---|---|
| -0xx | 0.070 (about 1/16) |
| -1xx | 0.103 (about 3/32) |
| -2xx | 0.139 (about 1/8) |
| -3xx | 0.210 (about 3/16) |
| -4xx | 0.275 (about 1/4) |

- Material follows Module 10: nitrile for oil, EPDM for water and steam, FKM for fuel and heat.
- 70 durometer is standard; 90 durometer resists extrusion at high pressure.
- Metric O-rings are sized ID × CS in millimeters and do not match inch sizes.
- Measure with an O-ring cone or calipers without stretching the ring. Better yet, measure the groove it sits in.

### Braided packing

Braided compression packing seals pump shafts and valve stems inside a stuffing box.

- **Packing size** = (stuffing box bore − shaft diameter) ÷ 2
- Cut rings to fit the shaft, and stagger the joints of each ring 90° from the last.
- Pump packing needs a small steady drip to cool and lubricate it. A dry packed pump burns the packing and scores the shaft.
- Materials include PTFE, graphite, aramid, and blends. Choose by media, temperature, and shaft speed.

### Mechanical seals

Many pumps use mechanical seals instead of packing. They are ordered by part number. Ask for the **pump make and model**, the **shaft or sleeve size**, the **seal type or part number** on the old seal, and the **media and temperature**.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: An O-ring kit, a coil of braided packing with cut rings, and a cartridge mechanical seal side by side]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: For any seal, the questions are the same as for hose: what it seals, how hot, how much pressure, and what it fits. Size alone is never enough.
