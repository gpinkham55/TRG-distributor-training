This last module covers three more product lines most branches sell: rubber expansion joints, belting, and fluid sealing (O-rings, packing, and mechanical seals). Each one is sold by the same method as hose: get every spec, match the material to the media, and confirm before you order. A missed spec on an expansion joint or a seal shuts down a pump or a pipeline the same way a wrong hose does. The module ends with the capstone scenarios and the final exam, which use every part of the course.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Gather the specs for a rubber expansion joint and explain control rods.
2. Read a conveyor belt spec and a V-belt part number, and know when belts are sold as a matched set.
3. Size an O-ring and braided packing, and list what to ask for a mechanical seal.
4. Quote and order these items in P21 with the specs in the extended description.
5. Work a customer request from first question to P21 order or quote, using every part of the course.

## 16.1 Rubber Expansion Joints

A rubber expansion joint is a flexible section bolted into a pipeline. It absorbs movement from heat, cuts vibration and noise from pumps, and takes up small misalignment. Mills, water plants, and HVAC systems use them on pump suction and discharge, on long hot runs, and where pipe meets equipment.

### Types

| Type | Where it fits |
|---|---|
| Single arch | General piping; pump suction and discharge |
| Multiple arch | Larger movements than a single arch can take |
| Filled arch | Slurry and abrasive media; the arch is filled so solids don't pack into it, and it takes less movement |
| Reducer (tapered) | Joins two pipe sizes |
| Offset or special | Built to a drawing for a misaligned or odd gap |

### How it is built

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Tube | The inner rubber layer that touches the media; chosen by media and temperature |
| Carcass | Fabric and wire layers that hold the pressure |
| Cover | The outer rubber layer that resists weather, ozone, and oil |
| Flanges | Full-face rubber flanges that seal against the pipe flange |
| Retaining rings | Split steel rings behind the rubber flanges that spread the bolt load |

Match the tube to the media the same way you match a gasket elastomer (Module 10): EPDM for hot water and steam condensate, nitrile for oil, neoprene for general service, chlorobutyl or FKM for chemicals per the maker's chart.

### Specs to gather

| Spec | Example |
|---|---|
| Pipe size | 6 in |
| Face-to-face length | 6 in |
| Flange drilling | Class 150 (Module 11) |
| Media, temperature, pressure, and vacuum | Water at 180°F, 100 psi, full vacuum on the suction side |
| Movements | Axial compression and extension, lateral, angular |
| Elastomer | EPDM for hot water; nitrile for oil |
| Control rods | Yes or no |
| Tag number | The customer's tag, when the plant uses them |

**Control rods** are steel rods across the joint that stop it from stretching too far when the pipe isn't anchored well enough to hold the pressure thrust. The customer's engineer decides whether they are needed.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A single-arch rubber expansion joint with control rods installed between a pump and its discharge pipe]

### Installation points to pass along

- Install the retaining rings behind the rubber flanges. Rubber flanges seal on their own; no extra gasket is used.
- Tighten bolts in a star pattern to the maker's bolt torque. Retighten only after the line has cooled, been depressurized, and been locked out, and only when the maker allows it.
- Keep the arch clean: no paint, no insulation over it, and no welding sparks near it.
- Set the joint at its face-to-face length. Stretching or squeezing it to fill a gap uses up its rated movement before the line starts.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A joint forced into a misaligned gap can fail and release the media. If the customer's pipe is out of line beyond the joint's rated movement, the pipe needs fixing first.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Mills and chemical plants often replace expansion joints during outages from a list of tag numbers. Keep the face-to-face length, drilling, and elastomer on file for each tag, and the next quote has all the information needed to price it accurately.

## 16.2 Conveyor and Drive Belting

Branches sell two kinds of belting: conveyor belt that carries material, and drive belts that carry power from a motor to a machine. Both are ordered by spec, and a wrong spec means a belt that wears out, slips, or won't splice.

### Conveyor belting

| Spec | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Belt width in inches | 24 in |
| Plies and rating | Fabric layers and the rated working tension in pounds per inch of belt width (PIW) | 2-ply, 220 PIW |
| Cover thickness | Top cover × bottom cover | 3/16 in × 1/16 in |
| Cover grade | Abrasion, heat, oil, or food resistance | Abrasion-resistant for gravel; heat-resistant for hot ash |
| Length | Feet off the roll, or endless length for a spliced belt | 150 ft |
| Splice | How the ends join | Mechanical fasteners (fast) or vulcanized (stronger, smoother) |

- Application: Ask what the belt carries, how hot it is, and whether oil or food touches it.
- Ask the pulley diameter. A thick, high-ply belt won't bend around a small pulley.
- Ask how they splice. Branches sell mechanical fasteners for the customer to install, and some branches install fasteners or vulcanize splices in-house. Fasteners are sized to the belt thickness and the pulley.
- Conveyor belt is usually sold by the foot off a roll. Check remnants for a piece that fills the need.

### Drive belts

V-belts carry power from a motor to a pump, fan, or machine. The part number gives the section and the length.

| Belt type | Sections | How to read the number |
|---|---|---|
| Classical | A, B, C, D | A48 = A section, about 48 in inside length |
| Narrow (wedge) | 3V, 5V, 8V | 5V1000 = 5V section, 100.0 in effective length |
| Light duty (FHP) | 2L, 3L, 4L, 5L | 4L480 = 4L section, 48.0 in outside length |
| Banded | Two or more belts joined across the top | 3/5V1000 = three 5V belts banded together |
| Cogged | Notched underside for small pulleys and less heat | AX48 = cogged A48 |

- On a drive with more than one groove, replace all the belts as a **matched set**. A new belt beside worn ones runs tighter, carries most of the load, and fails first.
- A worn belt is often stretched. Read the number off the belt; when it's worn off, measure the outside length and the top width. For a classical belt, subtract about 2 in (A), 3 in (B), or 4 in (C) from the measured outside length to get the belt number.
- Agricultural equipment often uses belts with the equipment maker's own part numbers. Cross-reference the number before you quote.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A section of conveyor belt showing the plies and covers, beside classical and narrow V-belts with their part numbers visible]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Belts and pulleys can pull in hands and clothing. Tell customers to lock out the machine before measuring or changing a belt.

## 16.3 O-Rings, Packing, and Mechanical Seals

### O-rings

An O-ring is sized by its **inside diameter (ID)** and **cross section (CS)**. Most inch O-rings follow the AS568 standard, which gives each size a dash number. For the -0xx (from -004) through -4xx series the first digit gives the cross section.

| AS568 series | Cross section (in) |
|---|---|
| -0xx | 0.070 (about 1/16) |
| -1xx | 0.103 (about 3/32) |
| -2xx | 0.139 (about 1/8) |
| -3xx | 0.210 (about 3/16) |
| -4xx | 0.275 (about 1/4) |

- Material follows Module 10: nitrile for oil, EPDM for water and steam, FKM for fuel and heat. EPDM swells and fails in petroleum oil.
- 70 durometer is standard; 90 durometer resists extrusion at high pressure.
- Metric O-rings are sized ID × cross section (CS) in millimeters and do not match inch sizes.
- Measure with an O-ring cone or calipers without stretching the ring. Better yet, measure the groove it sits in.
- Hydraulic fittings use their own O-rings: ORFS face seals and SAE straight thread ports (Module 7). Match the fitting's O-ring size and material.

### Braided packing

Braided compression packing seals pump shafts and valve stems inside a stuffing box.

- **Packing size** = (stuffing box bore − shaft diameter) ÷ 2
- **Number of rings** = stuffing box depth ÷ packing size, rounded down.
- **Length per ring** = (shaft diameter + packing size) × 3.14. Multiply by the ring count and add an allowance.
- Cut rings to fit the shaft, and stagger the joints of each ring 90° from the last.
- Pump packing needs a small steady drip to cool and lubricate it. A dry packed pump burns the packing and scores the shaft.
- Materials include PTFE, graphite, aramid, and blends. Choose by media, temperature, and shaft speed.
- Packing is sold by the foot or by the box. Ask how many feet or rings the customer needs.

| Example | Value |
|---|---|
| Stuffing box bore | 2.5 in |
| Shaft | 1.75 in |
| Packing size | (2.5 − 1.75) ÷ 2 = 0.375 in (3/8) |
| Stuffing box depth | 2.25 in |
| Rings | 2.25 ÷ 0.375 = 6 rings |
| Length per ring | (1.75 + 0.375) × 3.14 = 6.67 in |
| Total length | 6.67 × 6 = 40 in, about 3.3 FT before allowance |

### Mechanical seals

Many pumps use mechanical seals. A mechanical seal has a spinning face on the shaft and a stationary face in the pump, held together by a spring. They are ordered by part number. Ask for:

1. The **pump make and model**
2. The **shaft or sleeve size**
3. The **seal type or part number** on the old seal
4. The **media, temperature, and pressure**

A cartridge seal comes assembled and is easier to install. A component seal comes in pieces and is set up on the shaft. When the customer can't find a part number, ask for a photo of the seal and the pump nameplate.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: An O-ring kit, a coil of braided packing with cut rings, and a cartridge mechanical seal side by side]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: For any seal, the questions are the same as for hose: what it seals, how hot, how much pressure, and what it fits. Size alone is never enough.

## 16.4 Quoting and Ordering in P21

These lines are quoted and ordered the same way as hose and gaskets (Module 6). Most expansion joints, mechanical seals, and wide conveyor belt are special-order items, so the spec on the order line is what the buyer orders from.

| Product | Usual unit of measure | Usual disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Expansion joint | EA | S (special order) or D (direct ship) |
| Conveyor belt | FT | Stock for common widths; S for others |
| V-belt | EA | Stock for common sizes |
| O-ring | EA | Stock; kits for common sizes |
| Braided packing | FT | Stock |
| Mechanical seal | EA | S, by part number |

Check the item's unit of measure in Item Maintenance before you enter a quantity. Your branch's setup may differ.

### Steps

1. Search for the item by part number or description. For a special-order item with no stock item, ask your Manager which item to use.
2. Enter the quantity in the item's unit of measure.
3. Put the full spec in the line's **extended description**: for an expansion joint, size, face-to-face, drilling, elastomer, control rods, and tag number; for conveyor belt, width, plies, covers, grade, length, and splice.
4. For a quote, mark the order as a quote. Set the expiration 30 days out unless the customer asks for longer (Module 6).
5. For a special-order or direct-ship line, set the disposition. The buyer enters the PO to the vendor from the spec on the line.
6. Set up a customer part number when the customer orders the same item by their own number or tag.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Special-order expansion joints and seals usually can't be returned (Module 15). Read the full spec back to the customer and get it confirmed before the PO goes to the vendor.

## 16.5 Case Study, What If, and Rookie Mistakes

### Case study: an expansion joint for the outage list

A practice caller, a maintenance planner at Southeastern Paper Mill, emails an outage list. One line reads "6 in expansion joint, tag P-204 discharge."

1. The rep checks the customer part numbers and past orders for tag P-204. Nothing is on file.
2. The rep calls the planner and asks for the specs: 6 in pipe, 6 in face-to-face, Class 150 drilling, white water (paper machine process water) at 140°F and 60 psi, single arch, EPDM tube, movements per the maker's standard single arch, no control rods today.
3. The planner mentions the old joint is stretched and the bolts keep loosening. The rep asks how the pipe is anchored and suggests the planner have their engineer check whether control rods are needed.
4. The engineer calls back: add control rods.
5. The rep enters a quote with the item and the full spec in the extended description, including "tag P-204" and "with control rods."
6. The planner sends a PO. The rep converts the quote and sets the line to special order. The buyer enters the PO to the vendor.
7. The rep sets up a customer part number for tag P-204 with the spec in the item notes.

The next outage, the planner orders "P-204" and the order repeats.

### What if...

| What if... | Do this |
|---|---|
| The customer only has the size of an expansion joint | Ask for every spec in the 16.1 table before you quote |
| A V-belt's number is worn off | Measure outside length and top width, or get the number off a matching belt on the drive. For a classical belt, subtract about 2 in (A), 3 in (B), or 4 in (C) from the measured outside length to get the belt number |
| A customer wants one belt for a three-groove drive | Quote the matched set of three and explain why |
| The O-ring size is unknown | Measure the groove, or ask for the fitting or part it seals |
| The customer can't find a seal part number | Get the pump make, model, and nameplate photo, and a photo of the old seal |
| Conveyor belt is needed in a width you don't stock | Quote special order |

### Rookie mistakes

| Mistake | What it causes |
|---|---|
| Quoting an expansion joint by pipe size alone | Wrong length or drilling, and a special order that can't go back |
| Selling one V-belt on a multi-groove drive | A new belt beside worn ones runs tighter, carries most of the load, and fails first |
| Matching an O-ring by size and ignoring material | The ring swells or cracks in the media |
| Packing size guessed from the shaft alone | Rings too thin or too thick for the stuffing box |
| Spec left out of the extended description | The buyer doesn't know how to order |

## 16.6 Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Expansion joint | A flexible pipe section that absorbs movement and vibration |
| Face-to-face | An expansion joint's length from flange face to flange face |
| Control rods | Rods across a joint that limit how far it stretches |
| Retaining rings | Steel rings behind rubber flanges that spread the bolt load |
| PIW | Pounds per inch of belt width: a conveyor belt's rated working tension |
| Matched set | All belts on a multi-groove drive, replaced together |
| AS568 | The standard dash-number sizes for inch O-rings |
| Cross section | An O-ring's thickness |
| Stuffing box | The bore around a pump shaft that holds the packing |
| Mechanical seal | A shaft seal with a spinning face and a stationary face |
