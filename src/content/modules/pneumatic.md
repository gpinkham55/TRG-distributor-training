Compressed air runs equipment like tools, cylinders, valves, controls, and truck brakes. Pneumatic systems run at lower pressure than hydraulics and use their own hose, tubing, fittings, and couplers. The wrong part leaks or blows apart. This module covers the plant air system, air hose and tubing, tube fittings, quick couplers, air brake parts, and compressed air safety.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Trace a plant air system from the compressor to the tool.
2. Pick an air hose or tubing material.
3. Size tubing by OD and hose by ID, and tell inch tubing from metric.
4. Identify push-to-connect, compression, and barbed fittings, and install one.
5. Match quick couplers and plugs by interchange type.
6. Follow the rules for air brake (DOT) hose, tubing, and fittings.
7. Follow the safety rules for compressed air.
8. Find pneumatic parts in P21 and enter them with the right unit of measure.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never point compressed air at a person or use it to clean dust off clothing or skin. Air can enter the body through a cut and cause serious injury.

## 8.1 The Plant Air System

Air leaves the compressor, gets cleaned and dried, travels through pipe and hose to a drop, and reaches the tool or machine through tubing and couplers.

| Step | What it does | Parts a branch sells |
|---|---|---|
| Compressor | Pressurizes air to plant pressure, usually 90 to 125 psi | Compressor discharge hose, fittings |
| Receiver tank | Stores air and levels pressure | Drain valves, fittings |
| Dryer and filters | Removes water, oil, and dirt | Filter elements, tubing |
| Main lines and drops | Carries air through the plant | Air hose, fittings, ball valves |
| FRL (filter, regulator, lubricator) | Cleans the air, sets tool pressures, adds oil mist for some tools | FRL units, fittings, tubing |
| Hose to the tool | Connects the drop to the tool | Air hose, quick couplers and plugs, whip checks |
| Machine plumbing | Feeds cylinders, valves, and controls | Tubing, push-to-connect and compression fittings |

### Pressure and flow

Air tools need both pressure (psi) and flow (cfm, cubic feet per minute). A hose too small or too long for the tool drops the pressure at the tool, and the tool runs poorly. When a customer says a tool "has no power," ask the hose ID and length and the tool's cfm rating before you sell a new tool hose.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Ask what the air runs: a hand tool, a jackhammer, a cylinder, or a control. The answer decides the hose, the tubing, the fitting, and the coupler.

## 8.2 Air Hose and Tubing

### Hose or tubing?

|  | Air hose | Tubing |
|---|---|---|
| Built | Tube, reinforcement, cover | One layer of plastic, sometimes two |
| Sized by | Inside diameter (ID) | **Outside diameter (OD)** |
| Typical use | Tool hose, shop drops, compressor lines | Machine plumbing, controls, cylinders, air brakes |
| Fittings | Hose barbs, crimped fittings, couplers | Push-to-connect and compression fittings |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Tubing is sized by its outside diameter. 1/4 in tubing measures 1/4 in across the outside. Tube fittings grip the outside of the tube, so the OD has to match.

### Materials

| Material | Strengths | Limits | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rubber air hose | Tough, flexible in cold, handles heat and oil mist | Heavier | Shop air, compressors, jackhammers |
| PVC air hose | Light and low cost | Stiff in cold; weak with heat and oil | Light-duty shop air |
| Hybrid (rubber and PVC blend) | Light, flexible in cold | Medium duty | General shop air |
| Polyurethane tubing and hose | Flexible, kink resistant, abrasion resistant | Lower heat limit | Machine plumbing, recoil hose, robotics |
| Nylon tubing | Higher pressure, stiff, holds its shape | Less flexible | Machine plumbing, lubrication lines, air brakes |
| Polyethylene tubing | Low cost, chemical resistant | Lower pressure and heat | Instrument air, low-pressure lines |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Rolls of polyurethane, nylon, and polyethylene tubing next to a red rubber air hose]

### Inch and metric tubing

Inch and metric tubing look almost the same. Measure the OD with calipers before you sell a fitting.

| Inch size | OD (mm) | Closest metric |
|---|---|---|
| 5/32 in | 3.97 | 4 mm |
| 1/4 in | 6.35 | 6 mm |
| 5/16 in | 7.94 | 8 mm |
| 3/8 in | 9.53 | 10 mm |
| 1/2 in | 12.70 | 12 mm |

Each tube takes fittings made for its own size. 6 mm tube leaks in a 1/4 in fitting.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: European and Asian machines often use metric tubing. Ask who built the machine, and measure the tube.

### Pressure and temperature

Most plant air runs between 90 and 125 psi. A tubing's pressure rating drops as temperature rises. Check the maker's chart for tubing near ovens, dryers, or engines.

### Color coding

Tubing comes in many colors. Plants often color-code lines by circuit (supply, exhaust, cylinder extend, cylinder retract). Ask for the color when a customer replaces a line on a color-coded machine.

## 8.3 Tube Fittings

| Fitting | How it holds the tube | Strengths | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push-to-connect | A ring of stainless teeth grips the tube; an O-ring seals | Fast; no tools; easy to remove | Cut the tube square and push it in all the way |
| Compression | A nut squeezes a brass or plastic sleeve (ferrule) onto the tube | Strong hold; handles vibration | Use a tube insert in soft plastic tube; snug, then a set turn per the maker |
| Barbed | Barbs inside the hose, held with a clamp or crimped sleeve | Low cost | Hose only; lower pressure |

Most tube fittings have a thread on the other end: NPT, BSPP, or metric, often with sealant already on the thread. Identify that thread the same way as in Module 7.

### Fitting shapes

Straight, elbow, tee, union, bulkhead, and swivel versions cover most machine plumbing. Ask for the tube OD, the thread on the port, and the shape.

### Steps: install tubing in a push-to-connect fitting

1. Cut the tube square with a tube cutter. Side cutters crush the tube.
2. Check the end for burrs, scratches, or an oval shape.
3. Push the tube in firmly until it stops, past the grip ring and the O-ring.
4. Pull back on the tube to set the teeth.
5. To remove it, relieve the air pressure, push the release collar in, and pull the tube out.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A cutaway push-to-connect fitting showing the release collar, the grip ring, and the O-ring, with a square-cut tube]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Relieve the air before anyone disconnects a tube. A tube that comes free under pressure whips and can injure someone.

## 8.4 Quick Couplers

Quick couplers let workers connect and disconnect air tools in seconds. A set has two halves:

- **Coupler** (female, with a sleeve): usually on the air hose.
- **Plug** (male): usually on the tool.

### Interchange types

Couplers and plugs work only within the same **interchange type**. A plug of one type may go into a coupler of another type and leak, or fail to lock.

| Interchange | Also called | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial | Milton or M style, I/M | The most common type in North American plants and shops |
| Automotive | Tru-Flate or T style | Auto repair shops |
| ARO | A style | Some plants and tool brands |
| Lincoln | L style | Lubrication equipment |
| High flow (European) | V style or ISO B | Higher-flow tools; European equipment |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Industrial, automotive, and ARO plugs side by side, showing the different nose shapes]

### Coupler options

| Option | What it does | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Safety exhaust coupler | Vents the downstream air before the plug releases | Larger hose and tools; plants with a safety rule on hose whip |
| Push-to-connect coupler | Connects with a push, no sleeve pull | Production tools changed often |
| Sleeve lock | Locks the sleeve so it can't be bumped open | Tools dragged across the floor |
| Body size | 1/4, 3/8, 1/2 in bodies set the flow | High-flow tools need the larger body |

Order a coupler by interchange, body size, and the thread or hose barb on the back.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: When a customer asks for "a quick connect," ask which type their shop uses, or have them bring in a plug. The plug's nose shape identifies the type fastest.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Plants that standardize on one interchange type have fewer leaks and fewer lost tools. Some plants use a different type on purpose for nitrogen or breathing air, so those lines can't be connected to plant air by mistake.

## 8.5 Air Brakes, Air Safety, Industry Applications, and P21

### Air brake (DOT) parts

Trucks, trailers, and buses stop with air brakes. Brake hose, tubing, and fittings fall under U.S. Department of Transportation rules (FMVSS 106).

- Air brake hose, tubing, and fittings must be **DOT rated** and marked on the part.
- Air brake tubing is usually nylon made to SAE J844, sized by OD.
- DOT push-to-connect and compression fittings are made for brake tubing. Use only DOT fittings on a brake line.
- We build DOT air brake hose assemblies. FMVSS 106 requires each assembly to carry a label with our NHTSA manufacturer ID, *674, and the build date.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A failed brake line can cause a crash. Sell only DOT-rated parts for air brakes, and never substitute a non-DOT part to fill an order.

### Compressed air safety

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| Air used for cleaning is reduced below 30 psi, with chip guarding (OSHA 1910.242(b)) | Higher pressure drives debris or air into skin or eyes |
| Whip checks on universal couplings and large air hose | A loose hose whips with great force |
| Relieve pressure before disconnecting any line | Lines whip or blow off |
| Safety glasses and hearing protection around air tools | Flying debris and noise |

### Industry applications

| Industry | Use | Typical parts |
|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Valve actuators, machine controls, air knives | Polyurethane and nylon tubing, push-to-connect fittings |
| Agriculture | Planter and seeder air systems, truck and trailer brakes | Air hose, DOT brake tubing and fittings |
| Chemical | Valve actuators, instrument air | Polyethylene and nylon tubing, stainless fittings |
| Heavy equipment | Air tools, jackhammers, truck brakes | Air drill hose, universal couplings, DOT brake parts |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A machine control panel plumbed with color-coded polyurethane tubing and push-to-connect fittings]

### Pneumatic parts in P21

1. Press **F2** and search tubing by material and OD ("PU 1/4," "nylon 6mm"), fittings by type, tube OD, and thread, and couplers by interchange and body size.
2. Read the **UOM**: tubing in FT or by the roll, fittings and couplers in EA.
3. A customer replacing a full roll: confirm the roll length and enter it in FT, or the roll item in its own unit.
4. Check **net available** on every fitting. Machine plumbing jobs often need a dozen of one fitting.
5. For DOT parts, confirm the item description shows DOT before you sell it for a brake line.

## 8.6 Case Study, What If, and Rookie Mistakes

### Case study: the leaking control line

A maintenance tech brings in a cut piece of blue tubing and a push-to-connect elbow: "This line on the wrapper keeps blowing out. I need 50 feet of tubing and a dozen of these elbows."

1. **Measure.** The rep measures the tube OD with calipers: 6.0 mm. The wrapper was built in Europe.
2. **The cause.** The plant had been running 1/4 in tubing in 6 mm fittings. The grip ring never seated; the line blew out under pressure.
3. **The thread.** The elbow's port thread measures as 1/8 BSPP with a sealing ring.
4. **P21.** F2 shows 6 mm blue polyurethane tubing in FT and 6 mm by 1/8 BSPP push-to-connect elbows, 30 EA net available.
5. **Units.** The tubing sells in FT. The rep confirms the UOM and enters 50 FT.
6. **Order.** The rep enters the tubing and 12 EA of elbows, and adds a note to the account: "Wrapper on No. 2 line uses 6 mm metric tubing."

The tech asked for "the same as last time." The last time was the wrong size. Measuring the tube found the error and the account note keeps it from happening again.

### What if...

| Situation | What to do | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A customer asks for "1/4 in air line" | Ask hose or tubing, and measure the sample | Hose is sized by ID, tubing by OD |
| The tube measures 6 mm | Sell 6 mm tube and fittings | 6 mm tube leaks in 1/4 in fittings |
| A plug goes into the customer's coupler and leaks | Check the interchange type of each | Mixed types leak or fail to lock |
| A truck shop wants plant air fittings on a brake line | Sell only DOT-marked fittings and tubing | Brake lines fall under FMVSS 106 |
| A tool "has no power" | Ask the hose ID, length, and the tool's cfm | A small or long hose drops pressure at the tool |
| A customer wants to blow off clothes with shop air | Tell them it is unsafe at any pressure | Air can enter the body through skin |

### Rookie mistakes

| Mistake | What it causes | The habit that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Sizing tubing by ID | Fittings that don't fit | Measure the tube OD with calipers |
| Mixing inch and metric tubing | Lines that leak or blow out | Measure and ask who built the machine |
| Cutting tubing with side cutters | A crushed end that leaks | Use a tube cutter |
| Selling the wrong coupler interchange | A tool that won't connect or leaks | Match the plug's nose shape |
| A non-DOT fitting on a brake line | A brake failure | Check for the DOT marking |
| Entering tubing in the wrong unit | A short or long shipment | Read the UOM: FT or roll |

## 8.7 Pneumatic Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| psi / cfm | Pressure / airflow in cubic feet per minute |
| Receiver | The tank that stores compressed air |
| Drop | A pipe tap where tools connect to plant air |
| FRL | Filter, regulator, lubricator |
| Tubing OD | Tubing is sized by its outside diameter |
| Push-to-connect | A fitting that grips the tube with teeth and seals with an O-ring |
| Compression fitting | A fitting that squeezes a ferrule onto the tube |
| Tube insert | A stiffener inside soft tubing in a compression fitting |
| Release collar | The ring you push to free a tube from a push-to-connect fitting |
| Coupler / plug | The female / male halves of a quick connect |
| Interchange type | The coupler family a plug fits: industrial, automotive, ARO, Lincoln, high flow |
| Body size | The coupler size that sets flow |
| Safety exhaust coupler | A coupler that vents air before the plug releases |
| DOT | Rated and marked for air brakes under FMVSS 106 |
| SAE J844 | The standard for nylon air brake tubing |
| Whip check | A cable restraint across a hose joint |
