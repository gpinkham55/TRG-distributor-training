Compressed air runs tools, cylinders, valves, controls, and truck brakes. Pneumatic systems run at lower pressure than hydraulics, but they use their own hose, tubing, and fittings, and mixing them up causes leaks. This module covers air hose and tubing, tube fittings, quick couplers, and air brake parts.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Choose an air hose or tubing material for the job.
2. Explain why tubing is sized by OD and hose by ID.
3. Identify push-to-connect, compression, and barbed fittings, and install a tube in a push-to-connect fitting.
4. Match quick couplers by interchange type.
5. Know the rules for air brake (DOT) hose, tubing, and fittings.
6. Follow the safety rules for compressed air.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never point compressed air at a person or use it to clean dust off clothing or skin. Air can enter the body through a cut or the skin and cause serious injury.

## 8.1 Air Hose and Tubing

### Hose or tubing?

| | Air hose | Tubing |
|---|---|---|
| Built | Tube, reinforcement, cover | One layer of plastic, sometimes two |
| Sized by | Inside diameter (ID) | **Outside diameter (OD)** |
| Typical use | Tool hose, shop drops, compressor lines | Machine plumbing, controls, cylinders, air brakes |
| Fittings | Hose barbs, crimped fittings, couplers | Push-to-connect and compression fittings |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Tubing is sized by its outside diameter. 1/4 in tubing is 1/4 in across the outside. Tube fittings grip the outside of the tube, so the OD is what has to match.

### Materials

| Material | Strengths | Limits | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rubber air hose | Tough, flexible in cold, handles heat and oil mist | Heavier | Shop air, compressors, jackhammers |
| PVC air hose | Light and low cost | Stiff in cold; weak with heat and oil | Light-duty shop and home air |
| Hybrid (rubber and PVC blend) | Light, flexible in cold | Medium duty | General shop air |
| Polyurethane tubing and hose | Flexible, kink resistant, abrasion resistant | Lower heat limit | Machine plumbing, recoil hose, robotics |
| Nylon tubing | Higher pressure, stiff, holds its shape | Less flexible | Machine plumbing, lubrication lines, air brakes |
| Polyethylene tubing | Low cost, chemical resistant | Lower pressure and heat | Instrument air, low-pressure lines |

### Pressure

Most plant air systems run between 90 and 125 psi. Tubing pressure ratings drop as temperature rises, so check the maker's chart for hot areas.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Tubing comes in inch and metric sizes that look almost the same. 6 mm tubing is close to 1/4 in (6.35 mm) but will not seal in a 1/4 in fitting. European and Asian machines often use metric tubing.

## 8.2 Tube Fittings

| Fitting | How it holds the tube | Strengths | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push-to-connect | A ring of stainless teeth grips the tube; an O-ring seals | Fast; no tools; easy to remove | Tube must be cut square and pushed all the way in |
| Compression | A nut squeezes a brass or plastic sleeve (ferrule) onto the tube | Strong hold; handles vibration | Use a tube insert (stiffener) in soft plastic tube; don't overtighten |
| Barbed | Barbs inside the hose, held with a clamp or crimped sleeve | Low cost | For hose, not hard tubing; lower pressure |

Most tube fittings have a thread on the other end: NPT, BSPP, or metric, often with a sealant already applied to the thread.

### Steps: install tubing in a push-to-connect fitting

1. Cut the tube square with a tube cutter. Never use side cutters; they crush the tube.
2. Check the end for burrs, scratches, or an oval shape.
3. Push the tube in firmly until it stops (past the grip ring and the O-ring).
4. Pull back on the tube to set the teeth.
5. To remove, relieve the air pressure, push the release collar in, and pull the tube out.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A cutaway push-to-connect fitting showing the release collar, the grip ring, and the O-ring, with a square-cut tube]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never disconnect a tube under pressure. Relieve the air first. A tube that comes free under pressure whips and can hit someone.

## 8.3 Quick Couplers

Quick couplers let workers connect and disconnect air tools in seconds. A set has two halves:

- **Coupler** (female, with a sleeve): usually on the air hose.
- **Plug** (male): usually on the tool.

### Interchange types

Couplers and plugs only work within the same **interchange type**. A plug of one type may go into a coupler of another type but leak, or not lock at all.

| Interchange | Also called | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial | Milton or M style, I/M | The most common type in North American plants and shops |
| Automotive | Tru-Flate or T style | Auto repair shops |
| ARO | A style | Some plants and tool brands |
| Lincoln | L style | Lubrication equipment |
| High flow (European) | V style or ISO B | Higher-flow tools; European equipment |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Industrial, automotive, and ARO plugs side by side, showing the different nose shapes]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: When a customer asks for "a quick connect," ask which type their shop uses, or have them bring in a plug. Matching the plug's nose shape is the fastest way to identify the type.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Plants that standardize on one interchange type have fewer leaks and fewer lost tools. Some plants use a different type on purpose for nitrogen or breathing air, so those lines cannot be connected to plant air by mistake.

## 8.4 Air Brakes, Safety, and Industry Applications

### Air brake (DOT) parts

Trucks, trailers, and buses stop with air brakes. Brake hose, tubing, and fittings are covered by U.S. Department of Transportation rules (FMVSS 106).

- Air brake hose, tubing, and fittings must be **DOT rated** and marked on the part.
- Air brake tubing is usually nylon made to SAE J844, sized by OD.
- DOT push-to-connect and compression fittings are made for brake tubing. Never use a standard plant air fitting on a brake line.
- Building DOT brake hose assemblies requires the shop to be registered and to label each assembly. Ask your manager whether your branch builds them.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A failed brake line can cause a crash. Sell only DOT-rated parts for air brakes, and never substitute a non-DOT part to fill an order.

### Compressed air safety

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Compressed air used for cleaning must be reduced below 30 psi, with chip guarding | Higher pressure can drive debris or air into skin or eyes |
| Use whip checks on universal couplings and large air hose | A loose hose whips with great force |
| Relieve pressure before disconnecting any line | Lines can whip or blow off |
| Wear safety glasses and hearing protection around air tools | Flying debris and noise |

### Industry applications

| Industry | Use | Typical parts |
|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Valve actuators, machine controls, air knives | Polyurethane and nylon tubing, push-to-connect fittings |
| Agriculture | Planter and seeder air systems, truck and trailer brakes | Air hose, DOT brake tubing and fittings |
| Chemical | Valve actuators, instrument air | Polyethylene and nylon tubing, stainless fittings |
| Heavy equipment | Air tools, jackhammers, truck brakes | Air drill hose, universal couplings, DOT brake parts |

## 8.5 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Test-Out

### Practice

1. **Tubing.** Measure the OD of three tubing samples with calipers. Note which one is metric.
2. **Push-to-connect.** Cut a piece of tubing square, install it in a push-to-connect fitting, pull it to set it, then release it.
3. **Couplers.** Your mentor hands you 4 quick-connect plugs. Sort them by interchange type.
4. **DOT parts.** Find the DOT marking on a brake fitting and on a roll of brake tubing in stock.
5. **Counter call.** A practice customer brings in a leaking air line from a machine. Identify the tubing, the fitting, and the thread.

### Knowledge check (practice, not graded)

1. Is tubing sized by ID or OD?
2. Why can't 6 mm tubing go in a 1/4 in fitting?
3. What tool do you use to cut tubing for a push-to-connect fitting?
4. Which quick coupler interchange is the most common in North American plants?
5. A plug goes into a coupler of a different type. What can happen?
6. What marking must air brake fittings carry?
7. What is the pressure limit for compressed air used for cleaning?
8. Which tubing material is the most flexible and kink resistant?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your mentor.
