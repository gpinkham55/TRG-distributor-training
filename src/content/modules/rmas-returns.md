Customers return parts for many reasons: wrong part, ordered too many, damaged in shipping, or failed in service. A return handled well keeps the customer. A return handled loosely costs the branch money. This module covers return reasons, entering a return merchandise authorization (RMA) in P21, credit or replacement, restocking, and returns to vendors.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Sort a return by its reason, and know which items can and cannot be returned.
2. Enter an RMA in P21 against the original order or invoice.
3. Decide between credit and replacement, and when a restocking fee applies.
4. Receive and inspect a return and decide whether it goes back to stock.
5. Start a vendor return or warranty claim.

## 15.1 Return Reasons and Policy

| Reason | Example | Usual handling |
|---|---|---|
| Our error | We shipped the wrong part or quantity | Full credit or replacement; no restocking fee; often we arrange pickup |
| Customer ordered wrong or too many | Ordered -8 fittings, needed -6 | Return to stock if unused and resalable; restocking fee may apply |
| Damaged in shipping | Crushed carton | Freight claim (Module 13) and replacement |
| Failed in service | Hose burst after three months | Inspect; warranty claim with the maker if it qualifies |
| No longer needed | Project canceled | Your branch's policy; often a restocking fee |

### Usually not returnable

- Hose assemblies and other items built to order
- Hose, sheet, or cord cut to length
- Custom cut gaskets
- Special-order items the vendor won't take back
- Used or damaged items, unless the return is for a failure in service

Each branch sets its own return policy, time limits, and restocking fees. Your manager approves exceptions.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: The best return is the one that never happens. A complete spec and a read-back on every order (Modules 6 and 9) prevent most returns.

## 15.2 Entering an RMA

### Steps

1. Find the original **order or invoice** in the customer's sales history (Module 2). A return without one needs your manager's OK.
2. Open **RMA entry** in P21 and enter the customer and the original invoice.
3. Enter each item, the quantity being returned, and the **return reason**.
4. Choose the action: credit, replacement, or inspect first.
5. Add a note: who called, why, and anything the customer said about the part.
6. Save the RMA and give the customer the **RMA number**. Ask them to mark it on the package or bring it to the counter.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A hose returned from service may still hold fluid, pressure, or chemical residue. Ask what it carried, and handle it as dirty until you know otherwise (Module 1).

## 15.3 Credit or Replacement

| Option | When it fits | In P21 |
|---|---|---|
| Credit | The customer doesn't want the part again | The RMA creates a credit memo once the return is received |
| Replacement | The customer still needs the right part | Enter a new order for the replacement, linked to the RMA in a note |
| Advance replacement | The customer is down and can't wait for the return | Ship the replacement now on a new order; credit or charge when the return arrives and is inspected |
| Inspect first | A failure in service, or the cause is unclear | Hold the decision until the part is inspected |

**Restocking fees** cover the cost of handling and repacking a return the customer caused. They usually don't apply when the branch made the error. Your branch sets the amount; your manager approves waivers.

## 15.4 Receiving Returns and Restocking

1. Match the package to the **RMA number**.
2. Inspect each item: is it the item on the RMA, unused, in its package, and resalable?
3. Receive the return in P21 against the RMA.
4. Choose where it goes: back to stock, to the vendor, or scrap. Only clean, unused, resalable items go back on the shelf.
5. Add a note if anything differs from the RMA.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: A returned fitting that goes back on the shelf with a scratch or a missing O-ring becomes the next customer's problem. When in doubt, it doesn't go back to stock.

## 15.5 Vendor Returns and Warranty Claims

- **Vendor returns:** stock you bought and can't sell can sometimes go back to the vendor, usually with a restocking fee and a time limit. The buyer arranges it.
- **Warranty claims:** when a product fails in service and the cause may be the product, the maker may replace it. Keep the failed part, tag it with the RMA number and what happened, and send it to the maker as they direct. The maker decides.

| Information a warranty claim needs | Why |
|---|---|
| The failed part, not cut apart | The maker inspects it |
| Layline or part number and date code | Identifies the product and batch |
| Service details: media, pressure, temperature, hours | Shows whether it was used within its rating |
| How and where it failed | Points to the cause |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never promise a customer a warranty credit before the maker decides. Say you will send it in and call them with the result.

## 15.6 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Test-Out

### Practice in the P21Play System

1. **Return reasons.** Your mentor reads five return requests. Sort each by reason and say whether it can be returned.
2. **RMA for credit.** A practice customer ordered ten -8 JIC fittings and needs -6. Enter an RMA for the ten -8 fittings against the original invoice.
3. **Replacement.** For the same customer, enter a replacement order for ten -6 fittings and note the RMA number on it.
4. **Receive.** Receive the return against the RMA and decide whether the fittings go back to stock.
5. **Warranty.** A practice customer's two-month-old hose assembly burst. List what you need to start a warranty claim.

### Knowledge check (practice, not graded)

1. Can a custom-built hose assembly usually be returned?
2. We shipped the wrong part. Is there a restocking fee?
3. What do you give the customer after saving an RMA?
4. When would you ship an advance replacement?
5. A returned fitting has a missing O-ring. Does it go back to stock?
6. Who decides whether a warranty claim is approved?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your mentor.
