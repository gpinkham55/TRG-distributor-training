Every item a branch sells was bought first. Purchasing keeps stock on the shelf and gets special orders moving. Receiving checks what arrived, puts it where it belongs, and moves special orders on to the customer. This module covers purchase orders in P21, vendor lead times, special orders, and receiving.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Enter a purchase order with the right vendor, items, quantities, costs, and dates.
2. Explain lead time and promise dates, and follow up with a vendor.
3. Explain how a special order line creates a purchase need and links to the PO.
4. Receive a PO in P21, put stock away, and release special orders to ship.
5. Handle shortages, overages, wrong items, and freight damage at the dock.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Many branches have one buyer, and trainees don't place stock POs on their own. You still need to know how purchasing works so you can answer "when will it be here?" and receive what comes in.

## 13.1 Purchase Orders

A purchase order (PO) is your branch's order to a vendor. It tells the vendor what to send, how many, at what cost, and when.

### Steps: enter a PO

1. Open **Purchase Order Entry**.
2. Enter the **vendor** and confirm the vendor's ship-from location and contact.
3. Confirm the **ship-to**: your branch, or the customer's address for a direct ship.
4. Enter each **item** and **quantity**. Check the purchasing unit (a reel, a box, a sheet) against the selling unit.
5. Check the **cost** on each line against the vendor's price sheet or quote.
6. Enter the **required date** and, when the vendor confirms, the **promise date**.
7. Add notes for the vendor: tagging, test reports, packing, or delivery instructions.
8. **Save** and send the PO to the vendor by your branch's method.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Costs on a PO are private. Never share vendor cost with a customer, and never send a customer a copy of a vendor PO.

## 13.2 Lead Times and Special Orders

### Lead time and promise dates

**Lead time** is how long the vendor takes to ship after it gets the PO. The **promise date** is the date the vendor commits to. Customers hear dates from you; your dates come from the promise date plus freight time.

- Ask the vendor to confirm every PO with a promise date.
- Write the promise date on the PO line.
- Follow up before the promise date on anything a customer is waiting for.
- If a date slips, tell the customer right away, with the new date.

### Special orders

When an order line uses the **S (special order)** disposition (Module 6), P21 creates a purchase need for that line. The buyer turns it into a PO, and the PO line stays linked to the order line. When the PO is received, the stock is set aside for that order.

| Disposition | Who ships to the customer | Received at your branch? |
|---|---|---|
| S, special order | Your branch, after receiving | Yes |
| D, direct ship | The vendor, straight to the customer | No; the vendor's shipment is confirmed instead |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: For a special order, the customer's date depends on the PO. Check the PO's promise date before you give the customer a date, and check it again if they call.

## 13.3 Receiving and Putaway

Receiving is where P21 learns that stock arrived. Until a PO is received, P21 doesn't know the product is in the building, and the customer's order can't ship.

### Steps: receive a PO

1. Match the packing list to the **PO number** on the shipment.
2. Count every line. Check the item, quantity, and unit against the PO, not just the packing list.
3. Check for damage. Open boxes that look crushed or wet.
4. Open the **PO receipt** window in P21 and pull up the PO.
5. Enter the quantity received on each line. Receive only what is physically there.
6. Save the receipt. P21 adds the stock to inventory and allocates special-order stock to its order.
7. **Put away** stock items in their bin locations. Set special-order items aside, tagged with the customer and order number, for shipping or will call.
8. Hand the packing list to whoever matches vendor invoices.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A receiving bench with a packing list, the PO on screen, and a special-order item tagged with a customer order number]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Receive the same day the product arrives. A box that sits unreceived looks like a late order to the customer and a missing order to you.

### Matching the vendor invoice

Accounting pays the vendor when three things agree: the **PO** (what you ordered), the **receipt** (what arrived), and the **vendor invoice** (what you were billed). A wrong receipt creates a mismatch that holds up payment. Receive accurately and the match takes care of itself.

## 13.4 Problems at the Dock

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| Short shipment | Receive what arrived. Leave the rest open on the PO and tell the buyer. |
| Over shipment | Receive only what was ordered, unless the buyer approves the extra. Set the rest aside. |
| Wrong item | Don't receive it. Set it aside and tell the buyer, who arranges a return with the vendor. |
| Visible freight damage | Note the damage on the carrier's delivery receipt (bill of lading) **before** you sign. Take photos. Tell the buyer. |
| Hidden damage found later | Take photos, keep all packing, and tell the buyer right away; carriers set short deadlines for claims. |
| No PO number on the shipment | Search open POs by vendor and item before you set it aside. |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never sign a delivery receipt as "received in good condition" without looking. Once signed clean, a freight damage claim is very hard to win.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Reels of hose and pallets of sheet are heavy. Use the forklift or reel stand you are trained on, and never lift a reel by hand. Follow your branch's lifting and equipment rules.

## 13.5 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Test-Out

### Practice in the P21Play System

1. **Read a PO.** Open three open POs and write the vendor, items, quantities, and promise dates.
2. **Enter a PO.** Enter a stock PO for two items your mentor gives you. Check the purchasing unit on each.
3. **Special order.** Enter a customer order with an S line, find the purchase need or PO it creates, and note how they link.
4. **Receive.** Receive the PO from exercise 2, with one line short. Note what P21 shows for the open quantity.
5. **Dock problems.** Your mentor describes three deliveries: one short, one with a crushed carton, and one with the wrong item. Say what you would do with each.

### Knowledge check (practice, not graded)

1. What is the difference between lead time and a promise date?
2. A special order line is on an order. What does P21 create?
3. Who ships a direct ship (D) line to the customer?
4. Why receive product the same day it arrives?
5. A shipment arrives 2 short. What do you receive?
6. A carton arrives crushed. What do you do before you sign the delivery receipt?
7. What three documents must agree before accounting pays a vendor?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your mentor.
