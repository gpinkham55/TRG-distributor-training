Every hose you sell moves something: water, air, steam, oil, chemicals, grain, slurry. The wrong hose leaks, bursts, collapses, or dissolves, and the person standing next to it gets hurt. This module teaches how a hose is built, the STAMPED questions that pick the right one, and the ratings that tell you what it can take. Modules 5, 7, and 8 build on it for industrial, hydraulic, and pneumatic hose.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Describe the tube, reinforcement, and cover of a hose and what each one does.
2. Pick the reinforcement type for pressure, vacuum, and flexibility.
3. Run all seven STAMPED questions before you quote a hose, and read a failed hose for the answers.
4. Read hose sizes in inches and hydraulic dash sizes, and measure ID, overall length, and orientation.
5. Explain working pressure, burst pressure, safety factor, surges, vacuum rating, and temperature rating.
6. Explain why heat lowers a hose's life and pressure capacity.
7. Check a tube, fitting, and seal against the media on a manufacturer's compatibility chart.
8. Identify the standards behind hose: NAHAD, the ARPM Hose Handbook, SAE J517, and the EN hose standards.
9. Record a complete hose spec in P21 so the shop and the next rep build the same hose.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A hose assembly fails at its weakest point: the hose, the fitting, or the way they are joined. Never guess on pressure, temperature, or chemicals. When you are unsure, stop and ask your Manager.

## 4.1 How a Hose Is Built

Almost every hose has three layers, and each layer has one job.

| Layer | Job | Common materials |
|---|---|---|
| Tube (inside) | Holds the media; must resist what flows through it | EPDM, nitrile, neoprene, natural rubber, PVC, PTFE, UHMWPE |
| Reinforcement (middle) | Holds the pressure, or holds the hose open under vacuum | Textile braid or plies, wire braid, spiral wire, a steel or plastic helix |
| Cover (outside) | Protects against abrasion, weather, ozone, heat, and oil on the outside | Neoprene, EPDM, nitrile blends, PVC, polyurethane |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Cut hose ends showing the tube, the wire reinforcement, and the cover]

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: The tube decides what the hose can carry. The reinforcement decides how much pressure or vacuum it can take. The cover decides how long it lasts on the outside. A hose that fails early usually has one of the three wrong.

### Reinforcement types

| Reinforcement | What it gives the hose | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Textile braid or plies | Light weight, flexibility, low to medium pressure | Water, air, washdown hose |
| Wire braid (1 or 2 braids) | Medium to high pressure, good flexibility | Most hydraulic hose; steam hose |
| Spiral wire (4 or 6 spirals) | Very high pressure and pressure surges | Heavy equipment hydraulics |
| Helix (steel or plastic wire wound in a coil) | Holds the hose round under suction; resists kinking | Suction and discharge hose, material handling |

### Hose constructions

| Construction | What it is | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| Hardwall (suction) | Reinforced with a helix wire; holds its shape under vacuum | Tank trucks, pump suction, dredging, bulk material |
| Softwall (discharge only) | Textile reinforced, no helix; flattens when empty | Pump discharge, water transfer |
| Lay-flat | Woven jacket with a thin liner; rolls flat for storage | Fire hose, dewatering discharge |
| Crush-resistant | Heavy plastic helix that springs back after a vehicle runs over it | Farm and construction sites |
| PTFE-lined | PTFE tube with a braid or rubber cover | Chemical transfer, high heat, steam tracing |
| Food grade | Tube compounds that meet FDA rules; tube marked for food contact, often white | Food, dairy, beverage, pharmaceutical |
| Static-dissipating or conductive | Wire or carbon in the hose carries static to ground when the wire is bonded to both couplings | Fuels, solvents, dry bulk powders |

### Terms on the hose itself

- **ID (inside diameter):** the size of the opening. Most hose is sized by ID.
- **OD (outside diameter):** the size over the cover. It decides which clamps, ferrules, guards, and fittings fit.
- **Minimum bend radius:** the tightest bend the hose takes without damage. A tighter bend kinks the hose or shortens its life.
- **Layline:** the printing along the cover: maker, hose type, size, pressure, and often a date code.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Static builds up when fuels, solvents, and dry powders flow through a hose. A spark at the end of an ungrounded hose can ignite vapor or dust. When the media is flammable or a dry powder, confirm the hose is built to carry it and remind the customer to ground it.

## 4.2 The STAMPED Method

STAMPED is the checklist the hose industry uses to pick a hose. Ask all seven questions before you quote or build a hose. Customers usually give you two or three answers. You need all seven to pick the hose.

| Letter | Stands for | What to ask | What a wrong answer causes |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | Size | What ID? What overall length? | Restricted flow, a hose that won't reach, or fittings that don't fit |
| T | Temperature | How hot or cold is the media? How hot or cold is it around the hose? | A tube that hardens, cracks, or softens; lower pressure capacity |
| A | Application | What does the hose do, and where? Moving, bending, dragged, outdoors, under a guard? | A cover worn through, a kinked hose, a hose rubbed through on an edge |
| M | Media | What flows through it? Exact chemical and concentration? | A tube that swells or dissolves, or a fire from static |
| P | Pressure | Working pressure? Surges? Suction or discharge? | A burst, or a collapsed suction hose |
| E | Ends | What fitting on each end? What thread or connection, and what size? Orientation? | An end that won't connect or won't seal |
| D | Delivery | When is it needed? Testing, tagging, certs, packaging? | A hose that shows up late or without required paperwork |

### A STAMPED conversation

A practice caller says: "I need a hose for our washdown station."

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Size | 3/4 in ID, 50 ft long |
| Temperature | Hot water up to 180°F |
| Application | Hand-held washdown, dragged across a concrete floor |
| Media | Water with a mild cleaning chemical (the caller reads the product label) |
| Pressure | 150 psi from the plant water system |
| Ends | A 3/4 in garden hose thread female swivel on one end, a spray nozzle on the other |
| Delivery | Needed within 3 business days; tag it with the station number |

With these answers you can pick a hot water washdown hose with an abrasion-resistant cover, check the cleaning chemical on a compatibility chart, and pick the fittings.

### Reading a failed hose

When a customer hands you a failed hose, it answers most of the STAMPED questions.

1. **Ask what it carried, put on gloves, then handle it.**
2. **Read the layline:** maker, hose type, ID, working pressure, date code.
3. **Measure** the ID at a clean cut and the overall length end to end.
4. **Identify the ends:** fitting type, thread, size, and the angle between elbows.
5. **Look at the failure.** It tells you which STAMPED answer was wrong.

| What you see | Likely cause | STAMPED letter |
|---|---|---|
| Cover worn through to the wire | Rubbing or dragging | A |
| Hard, cracked tube or cover | Heat above the rating | T |
| Swollen, soft, or sticky tube | Media attacking the tube | M |
| Burst along the hose body | Pressure or surges above the rating | P |
| Blown off at the fitting | Wrong fitting, bad crimp, or a missing clamp | E |
| Kinked or flattened | Bent tighter than the bend radius, or a softwall hose on suction | A, P |
| Rusted wire under a damaged cover | Cover damage let water in | A |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Replace a failed hose with the same hose only after you know why it failed. The same hose in the same service fails the same way.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: A worn or missing layline means measuring and asking all seven questions. Never read a layline off a neighboring hose and assume it matches.

## 4.3 Sizing, Dash Sizes, and Measuring

Hose is sized by its **inside diameter (ID)**. The ID sets how much the hose can flow. Industrial hose is sized in inches ("2 in suction hose"). Hydraulic hose is called out in **dash sizes**.

### Dash sizes

A dash size is the hose ID in sixteenths of an inch. A -8 hose has an ID of 8/16 in, or 1/2 in.

| Dash size | ID (in) | ID (mm, approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| -4 | 1/4 | 6 |
| -6 | 3/8 | 10 |
| -8 | 1/2 | 13 |
| -10 | 5/8 | 16 |
| -12 | 3/4 | 19 |
| -16 | 1 | 25 |
| -20 | 1-1/4 | 32 |
| -24 | 1-1/2 | 38 |
| -32 | 2 | 51 |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: SAE 100R5 hose and some thermoplastic and tube products use dash sizes based on tube OD, so a -8 R5 hose has a smaller ID than 1/2 in. Check the manufacturer's catalog for every hose type.

### Hose size and thread size are separate

The fitting on the end of a hose has its own size. A -8 hose can end in a -6, -8, or -10 thread. Size the hose and the ends separately. Module 7 covers thread identification.

### Measuring a hose

1. **ID:** measure across the inside of a clean, square cut with calipers. Measuring the OD and guessing the ID gives the wrong hose.
2. **Overall length:** measure from the end of one fitting to the end of the other, along the hose. On an elbow, measure to the center line of the fitting's sealing face.
3. **Cut length:** the length of bulk hose before the fittings go on. The shop works it out from the overall length and the fitting dimensions. You give the shop the overall length.
4. **Orientation:** with an elbow on each end, sight down the hose with the far elbow pointing straight up. Read the angle clockwise to the near elbow.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A hose assembly with two elbow fittings, showing where overall length is measured and the angle between the elbows]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: When a customer gives you a length, ask whether it is the overall length with fittings or the hose alone. The answer changes the build.

## 4.4 Pressure, Vacuum, and Temperature

Match the hose to the customer's working pressure, highest surge, vacuum, and both temperatures. A hose rated below any one of them is the wrong hose.

### Pressure ratings

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Working pressure | The highest pressure the hose is rated to carry in normal use. Match it to the customer's system. |
| Burst pressure | The pressure where a new hose fails in a lab test. Use it only to check the safety factor. |
| Safety factor | Burst pressure divided by working pressure |
| Surge or spike | A brief jump above normal pressure, for example when a valve slams shut. Surges count against the working pressure. |

| Hose type | Typical safety factor |
|---|---|
| Hydraulic hose | 4 to 1 |
| Water hose on clean water at moderate pressure | 3 to 1 |
| Industrial hose on air, on abrasive or corrosive media, or at higher pressure | 4 to 1 |
| Steam hose | 10 to 1 |

A hydraulic hose with a 3,000 psi working pressure and a 4 to 1 safety factor has a 12,000 psi minimum burst pressure. The customer runs it at 3,000 psi or less, surges included.

### Units

| Convert | Rule |
|---|---|
| bar to psi | Multiply by 14.5 (200 bar = 2,900 psi) |
| MPa to psi | Multiply by 145 (21 MPa = 3,045 psi) |
| psi to bar | Divide by 14.5 |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: An assembly is rated at its weakest part. A 3,000 psi hose with a 1,500 psi fitting is a 1,500 psi assembly.

### Vacuum (suction)

A hose under suction must hold its shape. Suction hose has a helix to hold it open. Vacuum is rated in **inches of mercury (inHg)**. Full vacuum at sea level is about 29.9 inHg, which equals 14.7 psi pulling in on the hose.

- Ask whether the hose is on the **suction** side or the **discharge** side of the pump.
- A softwall discharge hose collapses on the suction side.
- Heat and a tight bend both lower a suction hose's vacuum rating. Check the catalog for the conditions behind the rating.

### Temperature

Check two temperatures on every hose:

1. **Media temperature:** how hot or cold the media is.
2. **Ambient temperature:** how hot or cold it is around the hose: near a dryer, a furnace, or outdoors in winter.

Heat shortens hose life and lowers the pressure many hoses can hold. A common rule of thumb: every 18°F above a hose's rated temperature cuts its life about in half. Cold makes rubber stiff; a hose flexed below its low rating can crack.

### Steam

Steam pressure sets steam temperature. The higher the pressure, the hotter the steam.

| Saturated steam pressure | Temperature |
|---|---|
| 50 psi | 298°F |
| 100 psi | 338°F |
| 150 psi | 366°F |
| 200 psi | 388°F |
| 250 psi | 406°F |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Use only hose rated for steam, with steam fittings and interlocking clamps or the ferrules the maker specifies. A water hose on steam can burst and cause severe burns. Steam hose needs regular inspection; ask the customer when theirs was last checked.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Paper mills run steam, hot water, and hot chemical lines everywhere. When a mill buyer asks for "a hot water hose," ask how hot and whether steam ever runs in the line. Many hot water lines carry steam during cleanup.

## 4.5 Chemical Compatibility and Hose Standards

The tube must hold up to the media. A tube that is wrong for the chemical swells, cracks, softens, or dissolves, and the hose fails. Check the fittings and any gaskets or O-rings in the line the same way.

### Common tube materials

| Material | Good with | Poor with |
|---|---|---|
| EPDM | Water, hot water, steam, many dilute acids and caustics, ketones | Petroleum oils, fuels, solvents |
| Nitrile (NBR) | Petroleum oils, hydraulic oil, diesel, many fuels | Ketones, strong acids, ozone and sunlight on the outside |
| Neoprene (CR) | Moderate oil, weather, ozone | Strong acids, ketones, aromatic solvents |
| Natural rubber and SBR | Water, air, abrasive slurries | Oils, fuels, ozone |
| FKM (often sold as Viton) | Fuels, many acids, aromatic solvents, high heat | Ketones such as acetone and MEK; steam |
| UHMWPE | A wide range of acids, caustics, and solvents | Some strong oxidizers; ask the manufacturer |
| PTFE | Almost all chemicals, over a wide temperature range | Very few; the outer layers and fittings set the limits |
| PVC | Light-duty water, air, some mild chemicals | Heat, most solvents |

Use this table as a starting point. Confirm every chemical on the hose manufacturer's chart.

### Steps: check compatibility

1. Get the **exact chemical**. When the customer has only a trade name, ask for the Safety Data Sheet (SDS).
2. Get the **concentration** and the **temperature**. A tube rated for a chemical at 70°F may fail with it at 180°F.
3. Look up the chemical in the **manufacturer's compatibility chart** for the tube material.
4. Read the rating. Charts rate each chemical excellent, good, fair, or not recommended, by letter or number.
5. Check the **fittings** and any **seals** the same way. A stainless fitting may hold where brass or aluminum fails.
6. When the chemical is rated not recommended, pick another material. When the chemical is missing from the chart or rated fair, stop and ask your Manager or the manufacturer.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A page from a hose manufacturer's chemical compatibility chart with one chemical row and its ratings highlighted]

### Examples by industry

| Industry | Media | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Black liquor, white liquor, caustic soda | Strong caustic, often hot; check concentration and temperature |
| Chemical | Sulfuric acid, solvents, mixed chemicals | Often needs a PTFE or UHMWPE tube and stainless fittings |
| Agriculture | Liquid fertilizer, herbicides, anhydrous ammonia | Anhydrous ammonia needs hose built and marked for it; follow the replacement date on the hose |
| Heavy equipment | Hydraulic oil, diesel, coolant | Nitrile tube for oil and fuel; coolant often needs EPDM |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never guess compatibility. A chemical hose failure can injure people and shut down a plant. When you can't confirm it on a chart, tell the customer: "I'll confirm that and call you back."

### The standards behind hose

| Standard | What it covers | When you see it |
|---|---|---|
| NAHAD Hose Assembly Guidelines | How to specify, build, test, tag, and document industrial and hydraulic assemblies | Customer quality requirements; shop procedures |
| ARPM Hose Handbook | Hose construction, tolerances, coupling methods, testing, care and storage | Catalogs; tolerance questions |
| SAE J517 | Hydraulic hose types, the 100R series (100R1, 100R2, 100R12, and more) | Hydraulic laylines and catalogs |
| SAE J1273 | Selecting, routing, installing, and maintaining hydraulic hose | Hydraulic application questions |
| EN 853, 856, 857 | European hydraulic hose types (1SN, 2SN, 4SP, 4SH, 1SC, 2SC) | Laylines on European and Asian equipment |
| FDA food-contact rules | Rubber compounds approved to touch food | Food and beverage hose |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Ask for three things on every chemical hose: the exact chemical, its concentration, and its temperature.

## 4.6 Hose in P21 and at the Counter

### Finding bulk hose in P21

1. Press **F2** and search by a word from the description ("steam," "suction," "R2") plus the size, or by the maker's part number from the layline.
2. Read the description: hose type, ID, working pressure, and maker.
3. Read the **unit of measure**. Bulk hose sells in FT.
4. Read **net available**. Remember that P21 counts total feet; a long continuous length needs a reel check.
5. Check the **Substitute** tab for approved alternates with the same or higher ratings.
6. Check the maker's catalog for the temperature range and compatibility before you quote.

### Recording the spec

A hose spec in your head is lost when you hang up. Write the STAMPED answers where the shop and the next rep will see them.

| What | Where in P21 |
|---|---|
| Media, temperature, and pressure | Extended description on the order line |
| Overall length and orientation | Extended description on the order line |
| Tag or test requirement | Extended description on the order line |
| A repeat assembly | A kit item with its components, built from this spec (Module 9) |
| The customer's own part number | The customer part number on the item (Module 6) |

### At the counter with a failed hose

1. Ask what it carried, put on gloves, then handle it.
2. Read the layline and measure ID, overall length, and the ends.
3. Ask the remaining STAMPED questions.
4. Press **F8** and check Customer Sales History. The assembly may already be on file from an earlier order.
5. Press **F2** and check the bulk hose and fittings.
6. Give the customer an honest ready time from the shop.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Treat a failed hose as chemical-soaked until you know what it carried. Hydraulic oil, caustic, and solvents stay in the hose and on the fittings.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Write the STAMPED answers on the order. The shop builds from what is written, and the next rep quotes from what is written.

## 4.7 Case Study, What If, and Rookie Mistakes

### Case study: the caustic unloading hose

A planner at Southeastern Paper Mill brings a failed hose to the counter: "Make me one just like this for the caustic unloading station. Today if you can."

1. **Media first.** The rep asks what it carried: 50% caustic soda. The rep puts on gloves, then handles the hose.
2. **Layline.** 2 in ID chemical transfer hose, EPDM tube, 150 psi working pressure, with a date code from six years ago.
3. **Failure.** The hose body is sound. The aluminum cam and groove fittings on both ends are pitted and one is cracked.
4. **STAMPED.** Size: 2 in ID, 20 ft overall. Temperature: caustic arrives at 140°F. Application: truck unloading, dragged across a concrete pad. Media: 50% caustic soda. Pressure: 100 psi pump discharge. Ends: 2 in cam and groove, female coupler on the truck end, male adapter on the plant end. Delivery: today.
5. **Compatibility.** The maker's chart rates the EPDM tube good for 50% caustic soda at 140°F and rates aluminum not recommended. The rep picks stainless steel cam and groove fittings.
6. **P21.** F8 shows the old assembly in Customer Sales History with aluminum fittings. F2 shows bulk hose and stainless fittings on hand. The rep writes the media, temperature, and "stainless ends only" in the order line's extended description.
7. **Shop.** The shop builds, tests, and tags the assembly by end of day.

The hose did its job. The fittings were wrong for the media from the first order. Repeating that order would have failed the same way.

### What if...

| Situation | What to do | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The customer only knows the size | Ask the other six STAMPED questions before you quote | A hose picked on size alone is a guess |
| The layline is worn off | Measure ID and length, identify the ends, and ask all seven questions | A neighboring hose may be a different type |
| A chemical is missing from the chart | Stop and ask your Manager or the manufacturer | A wrong guess releases the chemical |
| The customer wants water hose on a steam line "for now" | Decline it, offer steam hose with steam fittings and clamps, and bring in your Manager | A water hose on steam bursts and burns |
| System pressure is 2,000 psi with 2,800 psi spikes | Pick hose and fittings rated for at least 2,800 psi | Surges count against the working pressure |
| A suction line keeps collapsing | Check for a softwall hose, heat, or a tight bend | Only hardwall hose holds its shape under vacuum |
| A fuel or powder transfer hose | Confirm static-dissipating construction and grounding | A static spark can ignite vapor or dust |

### Rookie mistakes

| Mistake | What it causes | The habit that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Quoting on size alone | Wrong hose, a return, a second trip | Run all seven STAMPED questions |
| Measuring the OD | A hose one size off | Measure the ID at a clean cut with calipers |
| Confusing hose length and overall length | An assembly too long or too short | Ask which length the customer means |
| Reading the burst pressure as the rating | A hose run four times over its working pressure | Read working pressure |
| Ignoring surges | A burst on a valve slam | Ask about spikes |
| Checking only the tube on a chemical | Fittings or seals that corrode or swell | Check tube, fittings, and seals |
| Repeating a failed hose without asking why | The same failure again | Read the failure before you reorder |

## 4.8 Fluid Conveyance Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tube | The inside layer that touches the media |
| Reinforcement | The middle layer that holds pressure or vacuum |
| Cover | The outside layer that protects the hose |
| Helix | A steel or plastic wire wound in a coil to hold a hose open |
| Hardwall / softwall | Suction-rated hose with a helix / discharge-only hose without one |
| ID / OD | Inside diameter / outside diameter |
| Dash size | Hydraulic hose ID in sixteenths of an inch. SAE 100R5 and some thermoplastic hose use dash sizes based on tube OD |
| Overall length | End of one fitting to the end of the other. On an elbow, measure to the center line of the sealing face |
| Cut length | Bulk hose length before fittings go on |
| Orientation | The angle between two elbow fittings |
| Minimum bend radius | The tightest bend a hose takes without damage |
| Layline | The printing along a hose |
| STAMPED | Size, Temperature, Application, Media, Pressure, Ends, Delivery |
| Working pressure | The highest pressure a hose is rated for in normal use |
| Burst pressure | The lab pressure where a new hose fails |
| Safety factor | Burst pressure divided by working pressure |
| Surge (spike) | A brief jump above normal pressure |
| inHg | Inches of mercury, the unit for vacuum |
| Media | Whatever flows through the hose |
| Ambient temperature | The temperature around the hose |
| Compatibility chart | The maker's ratings of materials against chemicals |
| SDS | Safety Data Sheet: the maker's document on a chemical's hazards and handling |
| Static-dissipating | Built to carry static electricity to ground |
| Extended description | The order line field that carries the spec to the shop |

## 4.9 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Module Quiz

### Practice

1. **Cut sections.** Your Manager gives you cut pieces of three hoses. For each, point out the tube, reinforcement, and cover, and give the reinforcement type.
2. **Read laylines.** Read the layline on five hoses in stock. Write the maker, hose type, ID, and working pressure for each.
3. **Measure.** Measure the ID of three unmarked hoses with calipers. Give the ID in inches, and the dash size for hydraulic hose.
4. **Orientation.** Measure the overall length and the angle between the elbows on a stocked assembly.
5. **STAMPED interview.** Your Manager plays a customer who asks for "a hose for our tank truck." Ask the STAMPED questions until you have all seven answers, and write them in the order line's extended description.
6. **Compatibility.** On a maker's chart, write the rating for EPDM and for nitrile with diesel fuel, 50% caustic soda at 150°F, acetone, and hot water at 180°F.
7. **P21.** Press F2 and find a bulk steam hose and a bulk suction hose. Write the UOM, net available, and any substitutes.

### Knowledge check (practice)

1. Which layer of a hose decides what media it can carry?
2. What is the ID of a -12 hose?
3. A hose has a 250 psi working pressure and a 4 to 1 safety factor. What is its minimum burst pressure?
4. A system runs at 2,000 psi with 2,800 psi spikes. Does a 2,500 psi hose fit?
5. What keeps a suction hose from collapsing?
6. List the three things you need before you check a chemical on a compatibility chart.
7. Which tube material is a poor choice for diesel fuel: nitrile or EPDM?
8. Saturated steam at 150 psi is about how hot?
9. Where do you write the STAMPED answers so the shop sees them?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your Manager.
