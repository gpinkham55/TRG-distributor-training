Every hose you sell moves something: water, air, steam, oil, chemicals, grain, slurry. The wrong hose can leak, burst, collapse, or dissolve. This module teaches how a hose is built and the questions that pick the right one. Modules 5, 7, and 8 build on it for industrial, hydraulic, and pneumatic hose.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Describe the three layers of a hose and what each one does.
2. Use the STAMPED questions to gather what you need before you quote a hose.
3. Read hose sizes, including hydraulic dash sizes.
4. Explain working pressure, burst pressure, vacuum rating, and temperature rating.
5. Use a compatibility chart to check a hose tube against the media.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A hose fails at its weakest point: the hose, the fitting, or the way they are joined. Never guess on pressure, temperature, or chemicals. When you are unsure, stop and ask your mentor.

## 4.1 How a Hose Is Built

Almost every hose has three layers. Each layer has one job.

| Layer | Job | Common materials |
|---|---|---|
| Tube (inside) | Holds the media; must resist what flows through it | EPDM, nitrile, neoprene, natural rubber, PVC, PTFE, UHMWPE |
| Reinforcement (middle) | Holds the pressure, or holds the hose open under vacuum | Textile braid or plies, wire braid, spiral wire, a steel or plastic helix |
| Cover (outside) | Protects against abrasion, weather, ozone, and oil on the outside | Neoprene, EPDM, nitrile blends, PVC, polyurethane |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Cut hose ends showing the tube, the wire reinforcement, and the cover]

### Reinforcement types

| Reinforcement | What it gives the hose | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Textile braid or plies | Light weight, flexibility, low to medium pressure | Water, air, washdown hose |
| Wire braid (1 or 2 braids) | Medium to high pressure, good flexibility | Most hydraulic hose; steam hose |
| Spiral wire (4 or 6 spirals) | Very high pressure and pressure surges | Heavy equipment hydraulics |
| Helix (steel or plastic wire wound in a coil) | Keeps the hose round under suction; resists kinking | Suction and discharge hose, material handling |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: The tube decides what the hose can carry. The reinforcement decides how much pressure or vacuum it can take. The cover decides how long it lasts on the outside. A hose that fails early usually has the wrong one of the three.

### Terms you will hear

- **ID (inside diameter):** the size of the opening. Most hose is sized by ID.
- **OD (outside diameter):** the size over the cover. It decides which clamps, guards, and fittings fit.
- **Minimum bend radius:** the tightest bend the hose takes without damage. A tighter bend kinks the hose or shortens its life.
- **Static wire or conductive hose:** a hose built to carry static electricity to ground. Required for some fuels, solvents, and dry bulk powders.

## 4.2 The STAMPED Method

STAMPED is the checklist the hose industry uses to pick a hose. Ask all seven questions before you quote or build a hose. When a customer hands you a failed hose, STAMPED tells you what to look for on it.

| Letter | Stands for | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| S | Size | What inside diameter? What overall length? |
| T | Temperature | How hot or cold is the media? How hot or cold is it around the hose? |
| A | Application | What is the hose used for and where? Moving, bending, dragging, under a guard, outdoors? |
| M | Media | What flows through the hose? Get the exact name and concentration for chemicals. |
| P | Pressure | What is the working pressure? Any surges or spikes? Is it under suction (vacuum)? |
| E | Ends | What fittings or couplings go on each end? What thread or connection, and what size? |
| D | Delivery | When does the customer need it? Is there anything special for testing, tagging, or packaging? |

### A STAMPED conversation

A practice caller says: "I need a hose for our washdown station."

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Size | 3/4 in ID, 50 ft long |
| Temperature | Hot water up to 180°F |
| Application | Hand-held washdown, dragged across a concrete floor |
| Media | Water with a mild cleaning chemical (the caller reads the product label) |
| Pressure | 150 psi from the plant water system |
| Ends | A 3/4 in garden hose thread female swivel on one end, a spray nozzle on the other |
| Delivery | Needed within 3 business days; tag it with the station number |

With these answers you can pick a hot water washdown hose with an abrasion-resistant cover, check the cleaning chemical on a compatibility chart, and pick the fittings.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Customers usually give you two or three of the seven answers. Your job is to get the rest before you quote. A hose picked on size alone is a guess.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: When a customer brings in a failed hose, read the layline (the printing along the cover). It shows the manufacturer, hose type, size, and pressure rating. A worn or missing layline means you measure and ask STAMPED questions instead.

## 4.3 Sizing and Dash Sizes

Hose is sized by its **inside diameter (ID)**. The ID sets how much the hose can flow. Industrial hose is usually called out in inches ("2 in suction hose"). Hydraulic hose is usually called out in **dash sizes**.

### Dash sizes

A dash size is the hose ID in sixteenths of an inch. A -8 hose has an ID of 8/16 in, or 1/2 in.

| Dash size | ID (in) | ID (mm, approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| -4 | 1/4 | 6 |
| -6 | 3/8 | 10 |
| -8 | 1/2 | 13 |
| -10 | 5/8 | 16 |
| -12 | 3/4 | 19 |
| -16 | 1 | 25 |
| -20 | 1-1/4 | 32 |
| -24 | 1-1/2 | 38 |
| -32 | 2 | 51 |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: A few hose types do not follow this rule. SAE 100R5 hose and some tube and thermoplastic products use dash sizes based on tube size, so a -8 R5 hose is smaller than 1/2 in ID. Check the manufacturer's catalog for every hose type.

### Hose size is not thread size

The fitting on the end of a hose has its own size, and it often doesn't match the hose. A -8 hose can end in a -6, -8, or -10 thread. Size the hose and the ends separately. Module 7 covers thread identification.

### Measuring a hose

1. **ID:** measure across the inside of a clean, square cut with calipers. Never measure the OD and guess.
2. **Overall length:** measure from the end of one fitting to the end of the other, along the hose. On an elbow fitting, measure to the center line of the fitting's sealing face; your mentor will show you.
3. **Cut length:** the length of bulk hose before the fittings go on. The shop works this out from the overall length and the fitting dimensions. You give the shop the overall length.
4. **Orientation:** with an elbow on each end, note the angle between them. Lay the hose flat and measure the twist from one elbow to the other.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A hose assembly with two elbow fittings, showing where overall length is measured and the angle between the elbows]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: When a customer gives you a length, ask whether it is the overall length with fittings or the hose alone. The answer changes the build.

## 4.4 Pressure, Vacuum, and Temperature

### Pressure ratings

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Working pressure | The highest pressure the hose is rated to carry in normal use. Match this to the customer's system. |
| Burst pressure | The pressure where a new hose fails in a lab test. Never use it to pick a hose. |
| Safety factor | Burst pressure divided by working pressure. |
| Surge or spike | A brief jump above normal pressure, for example when a valve slams shut. Surges count against the working pressure. |

Typical safety factors:

| Hose type | Typical safety factor |
|---|---|
| Hydraulic hose | 4 to 1 |
| Most industrial water and air hose | 4 to 1 |
| Steam hose | 10 to 1 |

A hydraulic hose with a 3,000 psi working pressure and a 4 to 1 safety factor has a 12,000 psi minimum burst pressure. The customer can run it at 3,000 psi, not 12,000.

**Units:** pressure is usually given in psi (pounds per square inch). Metric equipment uses bar. 1 bar is about 14.5 psi.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: An assembly is rated at its weakest part. A 3,000 psi hose with a 1,500 psi fitting is a 1,500 psi assembly.

### Vacuum (suction)

A hose under suction must not collapse. Suction hose has a helix or other reinforcement to hold it open. Vacuum is usually rated in **inches of mercury (inHg)**; full vacuum at sea level is about 29.9 inHg. If the customer is pumping out of a tank, a pit, or a truck, ask whether the hose is on the suction side or the discharge side of the pump.

### Temperature

Every hose has a temperature range. Check two temperatures:

1. **Media temperature:** how hot or cold the media is.
2. **Ambient temperature:** how hot or cold it is around the hose, for example near a dryer, a furnace, or outdoors in winter.

Heat shortens hose life and lowers the pressure a hose can safely hold. A hose run at the top of its temperature range wears out much faster than one run in the middle.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Steam is far hotter than boiling water. Saturated steam at 250 psi is about 406°F. Use only hose rated for steam, with steam fittings and the right clamps or ferrules. A water hose used on steam can burst and cause severe burns.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Paper mills run steam, hot water, and hot chemical lines everywhere. When a mill buyer asks for "a hot water hose," ask how hot and whether any steam is in the line. Many hot water lines carry steam during cleanup.

## 4.5 Chemical Compatibility

The tube must hold up to the media. A tube that is wrong for the chemical can swell, crack, soften, or dissolve, and the hose fails. The same goes for the fittings and any gaskets or O-rings in the line.

### Common tube materials

| Material | Good with | Poor with |
|---|---|---|
| EPDM | Water, hot water, steam, many dilute acids and caustics, ketones | Petroleum oils, fuels, solvents |
| Nitrile (NBR) | Petroleum oils, hydraulic oil, diesel, many fuels | Ketones, strong acids, ozone and sunlight on the outside |
| Neoprene (CR) | Moderate oil, weather, ozone | Strong acids, ketones, aromatic solvents |
| Natural rubber and SBR | Water, air, abrasive slurries | Oils, fuels, ozone |
| FKM (often sold as Viton) | Fuels, many acids, aromatic solvents, high heat | Ketones such as acetone and MEK; steam |
| UHMWPE | A wide range of acids, caustics, and solvents | Some strong oxidizers; ask the manufacturer |
| PTFE | Almost all chemicals, over a wide temperature range | Very few; the outer layers and fittings decide the limits |
| PVC | Light-duty water, air, some mild chemicals | Heat, most solvents |

This table is a starting point only. Always confirm with the hose manufacturer's chart.

### Steps: check compatibility

1. Get the **exact chemical name**, not a trade name or "acid." If the customer only has a product name, ask for the Safety Data Sheet (SDS).
2. Get the **concentration** and the **temperature**. A tube rated for a chemical at 70°F may not hold it at 180°F.
3. Look up the chemical in the **manufacturer's compatibility chart** for the tube material.
4. Read the rating. Charts usually rate each chemical with a letter or number for excellent, good, fair, or not recommended.
5. Check the **fittings** and any **seals** the same way. A stainless fitting may be fine where a brass or aluminum fitting fails.
6. If the chemical is not on the chart, or the rating is fair, stop and ask your mentor or the manufacturer.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A page from a hose manufacturer's chemical compatibility chart with one chemical row and its ratings highlighted]

### Examples by industry

| Industry | Media | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Black liquor, white liquor, caustic soda | Strong caustic, often hot; check concentration and temperature |
| Chemical | Sulfuric acid, solvents, mixed chemicals | Often needs PTFE or UHMWPE tube and stainless fittings |
| Agriculture | Liquid fertilizer, herbicides, anhydrous ammonia | Anhydrous ammonia needs hose built and marked for it, with inspection and replacement rules |
| Heavy equipment | Hydraulic oil, diesel, coolant | Nitrile tube for oil and fuel; coolant often needs EPDM |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never guess compatibility. A chemical hose failure can injure people and shut down a plant. If you can't confirm it on a chart, the answer to the customer is "I'll confirm that and call you back."

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Ask for three things on every chemical hose: the exact chemical, its concentration, and its temperature.

## 4.6 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Module Quiz

### Practice

1. **Cut sections.** Your mentor gives you cut pieces of three hoses. For each, point out the tube, reinforcement, and cover, and name the reinforcement type.
2. **Read laylines.** Read the layline on five hoses in stock. Write the manufacturer, hose type, ID, and working pressure for each.
3. **Measure.** Measure the ID of three unmarked hoses with calipers and give the dash size.
4. **STAMPED interview.** Your mentor plays a customer who asks for "a hose for our tank truck." Ask the STAMPED questions until you have all seven answers.
5. **Compatibility.** Look up these on a manufacturer's chart and write the rating for EPDM and for nitrile: diesel fuel, 50% caustic soda at 150°F, acetone, and hot water at 180°F.

### Knowledge check (practice, not graded)

1. Which layer of a hose decides what media it can carry?
2. What is the ID of a -12 hose?
3. A hose has a 250 psi working pressure and a 4 to 1 safety factor. What is its minimum burst pressure?
4. A customer's system runs at 2,000 psi with 2,800 psi spikes. Is a 2,500 psi hose OK?
5. What reinforcement keeps a suction hose from collapsing?
6. Give the three things you need before you check a chemical on a compatibility chart.
7. Which tube material is a poor choice for diesel fuel: nitrile or EPDM?
8. Why can't you use a water hose on a steam line?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your mentor.
