Most gaskets a branch sells go between two pipe flanges. To quote one, you need the flange face, size, and pressure class. To help a customer stop a leak, you need to know how the gasket was installed. A gasket cut to the wrong class won't fit the bolts. A ring gasket on a cast iron flat face flange can crack the flange when the bolts are tightened. This module covers flange faces and classes, gasket dimensions, spiral wound gaskets, installation and bolt torque, and leak troubleshooting.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Tell raised face, flat face, and ring type joint flanges apart, and pick the gasket style for each.
2. Explain flange pressure classes and the standards behind them (ASME B16.5, B16.47, B16.1).
3. Read flange gasket dimensions and look them up for Class 150 and Class 300.
4. Describe spiral wound gaskets and when they replace sheet gaskets.
5. Explain the installation steps, the star tightening pattern, and tightening in passes.
6. Walk a customer through the common causes of a leaking flange.
7. Gather what you need to quote a flange gasket in P21.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never tighten or loosen bolts on a line under pressure or on a hot line. The customer's plant must isolate, drain, and lock out the line first.

## 11.1 Flange Faces, Materials, and Pressure Classes

A flange is a flat ring at the end of a pipe, valve, or pump. Two flanges bolt together with a gasket between them.

### Flange faces

| Face | What it looks like | Gasket |
|---|---|---|
| Raised face (RF) | A raised ring around the bore; the bolts sit outside it | **Ring gasket**: fits inside the bolts, no bolt holes |
| Flat face (FF) | The whole face is flat | **Full-face gasket**: covers the whole face, with bolt holes |
| Ring type joint (RTJ) | A groove for a metal ring | Metal ring gasket, bought as a finished part |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A raised face flange with a ring gasket and a flat face flange with a full-face gasket, side by side]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Flat face cast iron, plastic, and FRP flanges get full-face gaskets. A ring gasket leaves the outer face unsupported, and the bolts can crack the flange.

### Flange materials

| Material | Standard | Face | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon and stainless steel | ASME B16.5 (1/2 to 24 in), B16.47 (26 to 60 in) | Usually raised face | Most plant piping |
| Cast iron | ASME B16.1 (Class 125 and 250) | Usually flat face | Pumps, valves, older water lines |
| PVC, CPVC, FRP | Maker's standard, often matching Class 150 bolting | Flat face | Low bolt load; soft gaskets |
| Glass-lined and lined steel | Maker's standard | Flat or raised | Low bolt load; ePTFE or soft gaskets |

### Pressure classes

Each steel flange has a **class**: 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500, or 2500. A higher class means a thicker flange, more and larger bolts, and a higher pressure rating. The class is cast or stamped on the flange rim.

| Class | Where you see it |
|---|---|
| 150 | Most water, air, and low-pressure steam and chemical lines |
| 300 | Higher-pressure steam and process lines |
| 600 and up | High-pressure steam, boiler, and process lines; usually spiral wound or metal gaskets |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Class 150 and Class 300 flanges of the same pipe size have different bolt circles and bolt counts. A gasket cut for one won't fit the other. Confirm the class every time.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: To quote a flange gasket you need five things: pipe size, class, face type (ring or full face), material, and thickness.

## 11.2 Gasket Dimensions

| Dimension | Ring gasket (RF) | Full-face gasket (FF) |
|---|---|---|
| ID (inside diameter) | Matches the pipe bore | Matches the pipe bore |
| OD (outside diameter) | Fits just inside the bolts | Same as the flange OD |
| Bolt holes | None | Number, hole size, and bolt circle diameter |

The **bolt circle** is the diameter of the circle through the centers of the bolt holes. Bolt holes are 1/8 in larger than the bolt.

### Thickness

- **1/16 in** is the standard for smooth, flat steel flanges.
- **1/8 in** suits older, rougher, or slightly uneven flanges, and many rubber gaskets.
- A thicker gasket creeps more and can blow out. Use the thinnest gasket the flange condition allows.

### Non-standard flanges

Pumps, tanks, manways, and equipment covers often have flanges that match no chart. Measure the ID, OD, bolt circle, and bolt holes, or ask for a drawing, a tracing, or the old gasket. Module 12 covers cutting custom shapes.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: For a non-standard flange, have the customer trace the flange on paper or send a drawing. A tracing beats a guess.

## 11.3 Spiral Wound and Metal Gaskets

Above Class 300, on hot steam, and on many hydrocarbon lines, sheet gaskets give way to spiral wound gaskets (ASME B16.20). They are bought as finished parts by pipe size and class.

| Part | What it is | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Winding | A V-shaped metal strip wound with a soft filler | Winding metal: 304 or 316 stainless, or an alloy |
| Filler | Flexible graphite or PTFE between the windings | Graphite for heat; PTFE for chemicals |
| Outer (centering) ring | A solid ring that centers the gasket on the bolts | Usually carbon steel |
| Inner ring | A solid ring inside the winding | Required for PTFE filler and many high-class sizes; ask the customer's spec |

### Color code (B16.20)

The outer ring is painted by winding metal, with a stripe for the filler.

| Color | Means |
|---|---|
| Yellow outer ring | 304 stainless winding |
| Green outer ring | 316L stainless winding |
| Gray stripe | Flexible graphite filler |
| White stripe | PTFE filler |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Spiral wound gaskets are single-use. A compressed spiral wound gasket can't be reused.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Ring type joint (RTJ) flanges take a solid metal ring gasket (oval or octagonal) sized by ring number. Order it by the ring number stamped on the old ring or the flange.

## 11.4 Installation, Bolt Torque, and Sequence

A gasket seals when the bolts squeeze it evenly to the right load. Customers often ask how to install a gasket. Give them these basics and point them to the gasket maker's torque chart for exact values. Plants that follow ASME PCC-1, the industry guideline for bolted flange joints, use the same steps.

### Steps: installing a flange gasket

1. Isolate, drain, and lock out the line.
2. Clean both flange faces. Remove all old gasket material without gouging the face.
3. Check the faces for scratches across the sealing area, warping, or corrosion.
4. Use a new gasket every time.
5. Lubricate the bolt threads and nut faces so the torque turns into bolt load.
6. Center the gasket. Keep grease and sealant off the gasket unless the maker calls for it.
7. Snug the bolts by hand, then tighten in a **star (crisscross) pattern**.
8. Tighten in passes: about 30%, then 60%, then 100% of the target torque, following the star pattern each time.
9. Make a final pass around the flange in order (clockwise) at 100% until no nut turns.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: An 8-bolt flange with the bolts numbered in star pattern order: 1, 5, 3, 7, 2, 6, 4, 8]

### Why passes and a star pattern

Tightening one bolt all the way pinches the gasket on that side and leaves the far side loose. The star pattern and passes bring the flanges together evenly, so the gasket gets the same load all the way around.

### What sets the torque

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Gasket material and thickness | Each material needs a minimum and maximum load to seal |
| Bolt size and grade | Larger, stronger bolts carry more load |
| Lubricant | A lubricated bolt reaches the load at less torque |
| Flange class and condition | Plastic, cast iron, and lined flanges take much lower loads |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Torque values come from the gasket maker's chart for that material, flange, and bolt. Never give a customer a torque number from memory.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Many plants retighten flange bolts after the first heat cycle on steam and hot oil lines, following the gasket maker's instructions. Some materials must stay untouched once hot. Point the customer to the maker's instructions.

## 11.5 Troubleshooting a Leaking Flange

When a customer calls about a leak, ask what changed and work through the likely causes before you sell a different gasket.

| Symptom or clue | Likely cause | What to suggest |
|---|---|---|
| Leaks soon after install | Uneven bolt load or too little torque | Reinstall with a new gasket, star pattern, and passes |
| Gasket squeezed out or cut | Too much torque, or the wrong material for the bolt load | A material rated for that bolt load and class |
| Gasket swollen, soft, or cracked | Wrong material for the media or temperature | Recheck compatibility and the P×T chart (Module 10) |
| Leaks after heating up | Gasket creep or relaxation | A lower-creep material or thinner gasket; retighten when the maker allows |
| Leaks on one side | Flanges out of line or not parallel | Fix the piping alignment first |
| Scratch or pit across the face | Damaged flange face | Repair the face, or try a soft, conforming material such as ePTFE |
| Reused gasket | Gasket already crushed | Use a new gasket every time |
| Cracked cast iron flange | Ring gasket on a flat face flange | Full-face gasket on flat face flanges |

### Questions to ask

1. When did it start leaking: at startup, after heat-up, or after months?
2. What gasket material and thickness went in?
3. How were the bolts tightened: pattern, passes, torque, lubricant?
4. Was the gasket new?
5. What do the flange faces and the old gasket look like?

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Most flange leaks start with the installation. Ask how it was installed before you sell a "better" gasket.

## 11.6 Flange Gaskets in P21 and at the Counter

### Gather the spec

| Spec | Example |
|---|---|
| Pipe size | 4 in |
| Class | 150 |
| Face and style | Raised face; ring gasket |
| Material | 1/16 in compressed fiber, nitrile binder |
| Service | 120 psi saturated steam (about 350°F) |
| Quantity | 12 |

### In P21

1. Press **F2** and search for a standard gasket item by size, class, style, and material ("4 150 ring fiber 1/16").
2. A stocked standard gasket: check **net available** and enter it in EA.
3. An unstocked standard size or a non-standard shape: quote it as a cut gasket (Module 12).
4. Put the service (media, temperature, pressure) in the line's extended description so the shop cuts from the right sheet.
5. For spiral wound gaskets, include winding, filler, and inner ring in the description.

### At the counter

- Read the class and size cast on the flange or stamped on the old gasket.
- Measure an old gasket's ID, OD, and bolt holes. Old steam gaskets may contain asbestos: handle them as little as possible and ask your mentor.
- When the customer has only a pipe size, ask the class and face before you quote.

## 11.7 Case Study, What If, and Rookie Mistakes

### Case study: the leak after heat-up

A planner calls: "We put new gaskets on the 6 inch steam header last week. Two flanges started leaking once the line came up to temperature. I need better gaskets."

1. **The flanges.** 6 in Class 300 raised face, carbon steel. Steam at 200 psi, about 388°F.
2. **What went in.** 1/8 in compressed fiber ring gaskets. The planner doesn't know the torque; the crew used impact wrenches, working around the flange in order.
3. **P×T check.** 200 psi at 388°F plots in zone 2 for 1/8 in fiber: it needs an engineering check. In 1/16 in flexible graphite with a tanged insert, it plots in zone 1.
4. **The cause.** A thick gasket, uneven bolt load from tightening in order, and no passes. The gasket crept once it heated.
5. **The offer.** 1/16 in graphite ring gaskets with a tanged insert for 6 in Class 300, plus the maker's torque chart and the star pattern with passes.
6. **P21.** The rep finds the gasket item, checks net available, and writes "200 psi steam, 388°F, Class 300 RF" in the extended description.

A better gasket helped. Installing it with the star pattern and passes fixed the leak.

### What if...

| Situation | What to do | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The customer gives only a pipe size | Ask the class and face | Bolt circles and bolt counts change by class |
| A cast iron pump flange | Quote a full-face gasket | A ring gasket can crack a flat face flange |
| Class 600 steam | Offer a spiral wound gasket by size and class | Sheet gaskets are rarely rated for it |
| The customer asks for a torque number | Send the gasket maker's torque chart | Torque depends on material, bolt, lubricant, and flange |
| A flange leaks on one side | Ask about alignment | A gasket can't fix misaligned piping |
| The customer wants to reuse a gasket | Sell a new one | A crushed gasket won't seal again |

### Rookie mistakes

| Mistake | What it causes | The habit that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Quoting without the class | A gasket that won't fit the bolts | Ask size, class, and face every time |
| Ring gasket on a flat face flange | A cracked flange | Full-face on flat face |
| Thicker gasket "to be safe" | More creep, a blowout | Use the thinnest the flange allows |
| Giving a torque number from memory | Crushed or loose gaskets | Use the maker's chart |
| Selling a "better" gasket on an install problem | The same leak again | Ask how it was installed |

## 11.8 Flange and Gasket Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Raised face (RF) | A flange with a raised sealing ring inside the bolts |
| Flat face (FF) | A flange with a flat face all the way out |
| Ring type joint (RTJ) | A flange with a groove for a metal ring |
| Ring gasket | A gasket that fits inside the bolts |
| Full-face gasket | A gasket that covers the whole face, with bolt holes |
| Class | The flange pressure rating: 150, 300, 600, and up |
| Bolt circle | The diameter through the centers of the bolt holes |
| ASME B16.5 / B16.47 | Steel flanges 1/2 to 24 in / 26 to 60 in |
| ASME B16.1 | Cast iron flanges (Class 125 and 250) |
| ASME B16.20 / B16.21 | Metal gaskets / nonmetallic flat gaskets |
| Spiral wound gasket | Metal and filler wound together, with centering rings |
| Centering ring | The outer ring that centers a spiral wound gasket |
| Star pattern | Tightening bolts across the flange from each other |
| Pass | One round of tightening at a set percent of torque |
| ASME PCC-1 | The industry guideline for assembling bolted flange joints |
| Creep | Gasket flow under load and heat that loosens the joint |

2. **Look-ups.** Write the full-face gasket dimensions for 3 in and 6 in Class 150 flanges, and the ring OD and bolt circle for a 4 in Class 300 flange.
