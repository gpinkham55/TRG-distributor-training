Most gaskets a branch sells go between two pipe flanges. To quote one, you need the flange type, size, and pressure class. To help a customer stop a leak, you need to know how the gasket is installed. This module covers flange types and classes, gasket dimensions, bolt tightening, and leak troubleshooting.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Tell a raised face flange from a flat face flange, and pick a ring or full-face gasket for each.
2. Explain flange pressure classes.
3. Read the dimensions of a flange gasket and look them up in a chart.
4. Explain the star tightening pattern and why bolts are tightened in passes.
5. Walk a customer through the common causes of a leaking flange.

## 11.1 Flange Types and Classes

A flange is a flat ring at the end of a pipe, valve, or pump. Two flanges bolt together with a gasket between them.

### Flange faces

| Face | What it looks like | Gasket |
|---|---|---|
| Raised face (RF) | A raised ring around the bore; the bolts sit outside it | **Ring gasket**: fits inside the bolts, no bolt holes |
| Flat face (FF) | The whole face is flat | **Full-face gasket**: covers the whole face, with bolt holes |
| Ring type joint (RTJ) | A groove for a metal ring | Metal ring gasket, bought as a finished part |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A raised face flange with a ring gasket and a flat face flange with a full-face gasket, side by side]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never use a ring gasket on a flat face cast iron or plastic flange. The bolts can crack the flange. Flat face flanges get full-face gaskets.

### Pressure classes

Steel pipe flanges in North America follow ASME B16.5 (up to 24 in). Each flange has a **class**: 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500, or 2500. A higher class means a thicker flange, more and larger bolts, and a higher pressure rating. The class is cast or stamped on the flange rim.

| Class | Where you see it |
|---|---|
| 150 | Most water, air, and low-pressure steam and chemical lines |
| 300 | Higher-pressure steam and process lines |
| 600 and up | High-pressure steam, boiler, and process lines; usually spiral-wound or metal gaskets |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Class 150 and Class 300 flanges of the same pipe size have different bolt circles. A gasket cut for one will not fit the other. Always confirm the class.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: To quote a flange gasket you need four things: pipe size, class, face type (ring or full face), and material and thickness.

## 11.2 Gasket Dimensions

| Dimension | Ring gasket (RF) | Full-face gasket (FF) |
|---|---|---|
| ID (inside diameter) | Matches the pipe bore | Matches the pipe bore |
| OD (outside diameter) | Fits just inside the bolts | Same as the flange OD |
| Bolt holes | None | Number, hole size, and bolt circle diameter |

The **bolt circle** is the diameter of the circle through the centers of the bolt holes.

### Class 150 flange gaskets (ASME B16.5 flanges, B16.21 gaskets)

All dimensions in inches. Bolt holes are 1/8 in larger than the bolt.

| Pipe size | Gasket ID | Ring gasket OD | Full-face OD | Bolt circle | Bolts |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.84 | 1.88 | 3.50 | 2.38 | 4 × 1/2 |
| 3/4 | 1.06 | 2.25 | 3.88 | 2.75 | 4 × 1/2 |
| 1 | 1.31 | 2.62 | 4.25 | 3.12 | 4 × 1/2 |
| 1-1/2 | 1.90 | 3.38 | 5.00 | 3.88 | 4 × 1/2 |
| 2 | 2.38 | 4.12 | 6.00 | 4.75 | 4 × 5/8 |
| 3 | 3.50 | 5.38 | 7.50 | 6.00 | 4 × 5/8 |
| 4 | 4.50 | 6.88 | 9.00 | 7.50 | 8 × 5/8 |
| 6 | 6.62 | 8.75 | 11.00 | 9.50 | 8 × 3/4 |
| 8 | 8.62 | 11.00 | 13.50 | 11.75 | 8 × 3/4 |

### Thickness

- **1/16 in** is the standard for smooth, flat steel flanges.
- **1/8 in** suits older, rougher, or slightly uneven flanges, and many rubber gaskets.
- Thicker is not better. A thick gasket creeps more and can blow out.

### Non-standard flanges

Pumps, tanks, manways, and equipment covers often have flanges that match no chart. Measure the ID, OD, bolt circle, and bolt holes, or ask for a drawing or the old gasket. Module 12 covers cutting custom shapes.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: For a non-standard flange, have the customer trace the flange on paper or send the old gasket. A tracing beats a guess.

## 11.3 Bolt Torque and Sequence

A gasket seals when the bolts squeeze it evenly to the right load. Customers often ask how to install a gasket. Give them these basics and point them to the gasket maker's torque chart for exact values.

### Steps: installing a flange gasket

1. Clean both flange faces. Remove all old gasket material without gouging the face.
2. Check the faces for scratches across the sealing area, warping, or corrosion.
3. Use a new gasket. Never reuse one.
4. Lubricate the bolt threads and nut faces so the torque turns into bolt load, not friction.
5. Center the gasket. Don't use grease or sealant on the gasket unless the maker says to.
6. Snug the bolts by hand, then tighten in a **star (crisscross) pattern**.
7. Tighten in passes: about 30%, then 60%, then 100% of the target torque, following the star pattern each time.
8. Make a final pass around the flange in order (clockwise) at 100% until no nut turns.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: An 8-bolt flange with the bolts numbered in star pattern order: 1, 5, 3, 7, 2, 6, 4, 8]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never tighten or loosen bolts on a line under pressure, and never on a hot steam line. The customer's plant must isolate, drain, and lock out the line first.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Many plants retighten flange bolts after the first heat cycle on steam and hot oil lines, following the gasket maker's instructions. Some gasket materials must not be retightened once hot. Point the customer to the maker's instructions.

## 11.4 Troubleshooting a Leaking Flange

When a customer calls about a leak, ask what changed and work through the likely causes.

| Symptom or clue | Likely cause | What to suggest |
|---|---|---|
| Leaks soon after install | Uneven bolt load or too little torque | Reinstall with a new gasket, star pattern, and passes |
| Gasket squeezed out or cut | Too much torque, or the wrong material for the bolt load | A material rated for that bolt load and class |
| Gasket swollen, soft, or cracked | Wrong material for the media or temperature | Recheck compatibility and the P×T chart (Module 10) |
| Leaks after heating up | Gasket creep or relaxation | A lower-creep material or thinner gasket; retighten if the maker allows |
| Leaks on one side | Flanges out of line or not parallel | Fix the piping alignment; a gasket won't make up for it |
| Scratch or pit across the face | Damaged flange face | Repair the face, or try a soft, conforming material such as ePTFE |
| Reused gasket | Gasket already crushed | Always use a new gasket |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Most flange leaks come from installation, not the gasket. Ask how it was installed before you sell a "better" gasket.

## 11.5 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Test-Out

1. **Flange ID.** Your mentor shows you two flanges. Identify the face type, the pipe size, and the class from the markings.
2. **Look-ups.** Using the Class 150 table, write the full-face gasket dimensions for 3 in and 6 in flanges.
