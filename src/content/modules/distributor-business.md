A rubber, hose, and gasket distributor stocks parts close to the customer, builds assemblies and cuts gaskets to order, and answers technical questions. This module covers how the business works, who buys from us, the standards and materials we work with, how an order moves from request to invoice, and how a sale moves through Prophet 21 (P21).

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Explain what value a distributor adds between the manufacturer and the end user.
2. Describe the main customer types, how each one buys, and how each one pays.
3. Describe where our products are used in pulp and paper, agriculture, chemical manufacturing, and heavy equipment.
4. Identify the industry standards (NAHAD, ARPM, ASTM, ASME, SAE, OSHA) and what each one covers.
5. Match the common rubber and plastic materials, where they are used and where they should be avoided.
6. Follow a customer request through the business from call to delivery.
7. Trace one sale through the P21 windows used at each step.
8. Spot the most common rookie mistakes and the safety hazards common to our industry.

## 1.1 What a Distributor Does

A distributor buys products from manufacturers in bulk, stocks them near the customer, adds value by building and cutting parts to order, and sells them in the quantities and on the timeline the customer needs.

| Link in the chain | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer | Makes bulk hose, fittings, gasket sheet, belting, seals | A hose mill sells hydraulic hose on 250 ft reels |
| Distributor | Stocks a wide range locally, builds and cuts to order, gives technical help, delivers | The branch cuts 6.5 ft of hose, crimps two fittings, tests it, and ships it the same day |
| End user | Installs and runs the product | A paper mill maintenance crew replaces a burst hose on a press |

Manufacturers sell in bulk. The distributor sells one hose, one gasket, or one fitting when the customer needs it.

### Case study: one burst hose

A practice caller, a maintenance planner at Southeastern Paper Mill, calls. A hose on the No. 2 paper machine burst and the machine is down. Here is what each person does.

1. The inside sales rep confirms the account and the ship-to, then asks the STAMPED questions: size, temperature, application, media, pressure, ends, and delivery.
2. The planner reads the layline from the failed hose. It is a 1 in steam hose with ground joint couplings.
3. The rep checks stock in P21: bulk steam hose is on the shelf, and so are the couplings and interlocking clamps.
4. The rep enters the order, sends an order acknowledgement to the customer, and prints the pick ticket for the shop and warehouse.
5. The shop builds and tests the assembly and tags it. The warehouse stages it for delivery.
6. The driver delivers the assembly. The sale goes into the customer's sales history, and the next reorder starts from that record.

Each step depended on the one before it. A wrong layline reading or a missed clamp would have sent the wrong hose and kept the machine down.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Get the spec right, build it right, and deliver it when you said you would. Customers reorder from the branch that does all three every time.

## 1.2 Customers, Markets, and How They Buy

Distributors serve seven main customer types, and each one buys differently: what they value, who places the order, and how they pay.

| Customer type | Who places the order | What they value most | Typical request |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant maintenance (MRO) | Maintenance planner, storeroom buyer, purchasing | Speed, the right part, uptime | "Our press hose blew. We need one built today." |
| Manufacturer (OEM) | Purchasing, production planning | Consistent quality, on-time repeat deliveries, price | "Ship 2,000 of part #4471 gaskets every month." |
| Agriculture | Farm owner, operator, equipment dealer | Fast turnaround in planting and harvest, field-ready parts | "My sprayer hose split. Can you make one while I wait?" |
| Construction and equipment | Mechanic, fleet manager, contractor | Getting machines back to work | "The excavator's boom hose is out on the job site." |
| Municipal and utilities | Fleet or plant supervisor, city purchasing | Correct paperwork, contract pricing, reliability | "We need a quote for the sanitation department on their blanket PO." |
| Other distributors and resellers | Buyer at the other company | Price, availability, fast shipping | "I need all the 2 inch suction hose you have in stock. What's the best price you can give me?" |
| Walk-in and cash customers | Anyone at the counter | Speed and a straight answer | "You got a ninety degree connector to connect an air hose to a vacuum pump?" |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: **MRO** (maintenance, repair, and operations) customers buy to keep equipment running, often in a hurry. **OEM** (original equipment manufacturer) customers buy parts that go into the products they build, usually on a schedule. Most branches serve both and handle them very differently.

### How requests arrive

| Channel | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Phone call | The most common channel for urgent requests; the caller often describes the part by what it does |
| Text message | Quick photos of a fitting or a hose layline; confirm the details before you act |
| Email | Purchase orders, quote requests, drawings, and spec sheets |
| Counter walk-in | The customer brings the old part; measure it, identify it, and match it |
| Outside sales rep | The rep brings in a quote request or order from a customer visit |

Every request, from any channel, follows the same path: identify the customer, understand the need, find or build the part, and confirm price, quantity, and delivery.

### How customers pay

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Purchase order (PO) | The customer's written authority to buy; most businesses require one on every order and invoice |
| Blanket PO and release | One PO covers many orders over time; each shipment carries a release number |
| Net terms (Net 30, Net 60) | The invoice is due 30 or 60 days after it is issued |
| Discount terms (2% 15, Net 60) | 2% off if paid within 15 days, otherwise due in 60 |
| COD (cash on delivery) | Paid by credit or debit card only, at pickup or before delivery. The branch takes no cash |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never promise to ship to a customer whose account is on credit hold without approval from accounting or your Manager. Take the order, explain politely that you need to check one thing, and route it to accounting. When accounting is unavailable, bring in your Manager.

### The four industries

This course draws its examples from four industries that use large amounts of hose, gaskets, seals, and belting.

| Industry | Operating conditions | Busiest times | What a down part costs | Products used most |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Steam, hot water, caustic and bleach chemicals, abrasive slurries; runs around the clock | Planned outages, when hundreds of parts are replaced at once | Lost production on a machine that never normally stops | Steam hose, PTFE and graphite gaskets, expansion joints, belting |
| Agriculture | Water, fertilizer and chemical sprays, grain dust, field hydraulics, weather | Planting and harvest seasons | A missed planting or harvest window | Suction and spray hose, hydraulic assemblies, V-belts, cam and groove |
| Chemical manufacturing | Acids, caustics, solvents, high temperatures; strict safety and environmental rules | Planned turnarounds | Safety exposure, product loss, regulatory reporting | PTFE-lined hose, PTFE and FKM gaskets, mechanical seals |
| Heavy equipment and construction | High-pressure hydraulics, vibration, dirt, impact | Construction season; any day a machine breaks | An idle machine and crew on a job site | Spiral and braided hydraulic assemblies, adapters, seals |

## 1.3 Standards and Materials Every Branch Works With

Hose assemblies and gaskets are built to published standards, and customers often list a standard on their PO. Learn what each one covers so you recognize it on a drawing, a spec sheet, or a layline.

### The standards

| Standard or body | What it covers | Where you will see it |
|---|---|---|
| NAHAD (Association for Hose and Accessories Distribution) | Hose assembly guidelines for specifying, building, testing, tagging, and documenting industrial and hydraulic assemblies | Shop procedures, test certificates, customer quality requirements |
| ARPM (Association for Rubber Products Manufacturers, formerly the RMA hose and rubber groups) | The Hose Handbook and Sheet Rubber Handbook: hose construction, tolerances, coupling methods, sheet rubber grades and tolerances | Manufacturer catalogs, tolerance questions, customer specs |
| ASTM D2000 | A coding system for rubber compounds: type, heat resistance, oil swell, hardness, and tests | Gasket and molded part drawings ("ASTM D2000 M2BG714") |
| ASTM F104 | A classification system for nonmetallic gasket materials such as fiber sheet | Gasket sheet data sheets |
| ASME B16.5 | Pipe flanges NPS 1/2 through 24 in pressure classes 150 to 2500 | Flange size and bolt pattern for every standard gasket |
| ASME B16.47 | Large-diameter flanges NPS 26 through 60 (Series A and B) | Large mill and plant piping |
| ASME B16.20 and B16.21 | Metallic gaskets (spiral wound, ring joint) and nonmetallic flat gaskets | Gasket dimensions and tolerances |
| SAE J517 and SAE J1273 | Hydraulic hose types (the 100R series) and the recommended practice for selecting, routing, and installing hydraulic hose | Hydraulic laylines and catalogs |
| EN 853, EN 856, EN 857 | European hydraulic hose types (1SN, 2SN, 4SP, 4SH, 1SC, 2SC) | Laylines on European and Asian equipment |
| OSHA 29 CFR 1910 | Workplace safety: eye protection (1910.133), lockout/tagout (1910.147), forklifts (1910.178), hazard communication and SDS (1910.1200) | Branch safety rules and customer site rules |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: When a customer puts a standard on a PO, it is part of the order. A PO that says "build and test to NAHAD guidelines" or "gaskets per ASME B16.21" means the finished product must meet the spec.

### Materials

Almost every hose tube, cover, gasket, and seal is made from one of these materials. The material decides what media and temperature the product can handle. Later modules go deeper; for now, learn what each one is good for and where it fails.

| Material | Also called | Good for | Weak against | Typical service temperature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Natural rubber (NR) | Gum rubber | Abrasion, water, slurries, mild chemicals | Oil, fuel, ozone, sunlight | -40 to 160°F |
| SBR | Red rubber | General-purpose water and air, low cost | Oil, fuel, ozone | -40 to 200°F |
| Nitrile (NBR) | Buna-N | Oil, fuel, hydraulic fluid | Ozone and weather, ketones, strong acids | -40 to 250°F |
| Neoprene (CR) | Chloroprene | Weather, ozone, moderate oil, general service | Strong acids, aromatic solvents | -40 to 225°F |
| EPDM | Ethylene propylene | Hot water, steam (steam grades), weather, many acids and caustics | Petroleum oil and fuel | -60 to 300°F |
| FKM | Viton (a brand) | Fuel, oil, many acids, high heat | Ketones, some steam and hot water service, amines | -15 to 400°F |
| Silicone | VMQ | Very wide temperature range, food and pharmaceutical service | Tearing, abrasion, fuel, steam under pressure | -80 to 450°F |
| PTFE | Teflon (a brand) | Nearly every chemical, wide temperature range | Cold flow (creep) under bolt load, stiffness | -100 to 500°F |
| PVC | Vinyl | Water, air, light chemicals, low cost | Heat, many solvents | 25 to 150°F |

Temperature ranges are typical and vary by vendor and product. When temperature is part of the application, check the rating of the exact product you are supplying. Hoses differ between manufacturers, and shelf stock may come from one maker on one buy and another maker on the next.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: The most common material mistake is EPDM in oil service. EPDM swells and fails in petroleum oil and fuel. Nitrile is the opposite: good in oil, poor outdoors in sun and ozone. Never substitute one for the other without checking the media.

### STAMPED: the questions for every hose request

Every hose request starts with the same seven questions. Module 4 teaches each one in depth.

| Letter | Question | Example answer |
|---|---|---|
| S | Size: inside diameter and length | 1 in ID, 12 ft overall |
| T | Temperature of the media and the surroundings | 366°F steam |
| A | Application: what it does and how it is used | Press clean-up line, dragged on the floor |
| M | Media: what flows through it | Saturated steam |
| P | Pressure, including surges | 150 psi working |
| E | Ends: couplings and how they attach | Ground joint couplings, interlocking clamps |
| D | Delivery: when and how it gets there | Same day, branch truck |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY:  STAMPED tells you what the customer needs. Standards tell you how to build it. Materials tell you what it can survive. You need all three before you quote.

## 1.4 Branch Roles and the Daily Workflow

Every request passes through several hands before it becomes an invoice.

### Who does what

| Role | Main job | What they need from you |
|---|---|---|
| Counter sales | Serves walk-in customers, identifies parts, sells from stock, writes will-call orders | A clear spec and the right account before the sale |
| Inside sales and customer service | Answers phone and email, quotes, enters orders, follows up | Complete orders: PO, ship-to, contact, required date |
| Outside sales | Visits customers, finds new business, brings in quotes and orders | Fast, accurate quotes and updates on open orders |
| Warehouse | Receives, puts away, picks, stages, ships, counts | Correct item IDs, quantities, and units on every order |
| Shop and fabrication | Builds assemblies, cuts gaskets and sheet, tests and tags | A complete spec and a realistic promise date |
| Purchasing | Buys stock and special orders, manages vendors, arranges vendor returns | Special-order details and the customer's required date |
| Delivery driver | Runs the branch truck route, collects signatures, brings back returns | Orders staged by route with correct paperwork |
| Branch Manager | Approves credit releases, pricing exceptions, returns, and adjustments | The facts, early, when something is outside the normal path |

### The order life cycle

Every order follows the same steps. The source changes: the shelf, the shop, another branch, or a vendor.

1. **Request.** A call, email, text, counter visit, or sales rep brings in a need.
2. **Identify.** Confirm the customer account, ship-to, and contact.
3. **Specify.** Get the full spec: STAMPED for hose, dimensions and material for gaskets, part number or old part for everything else.
4. **Check.** Look up stock, price, and lead time.
5. **Quote or order.** A quote when the customer wants a price first; an order when they are ready to buy.
6. **Source.** Pick from stock, build in the shop, transfer from another branch, or buy from a vendor (special order or direct ship).
7. **Stage.** Pull, check, and stage the order by route, carrier, or will call.
8. **Deliver.** Branch truck, freight, parcel, or counter pickup, with a signed delivery record.
9. **Invoice.** P21 invoices the shipped quantity.
10. **Follow up.** Confirm the customer has what they need and note anything for next time.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Order sourcing: one saved order fills from stock, the shop, a transfer, a special order, or a direct ship, and every path ends in one invoice]

Stock, shop, transfer, and special-order lines all pass through the branch; a direct-ship line goes from the vendor to the customer and still invoices from the branch.

### Counter sales

Handle every counter sale in this order:

1. Greet the customer and ask for the account name or number. Cash customers use the branch's cash sales account.
2. Ask what the part does before you ask what it is. A customer holding a failed hose knows where it went and what it carried.
3. Measure and identify the old part: ID, length, fittings, threads, layline. Never guess a thread by eye.
4. Check stock and price in P21. If it needs building, give an honest ready time from the shop.
5. Enter the order, take payment or confirm the PO, and print the paperwork.
6. Read the order back before the customer leaves.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Treat a customer's failed hose as chemical-soaked until you know what it carried. Ask before you handle it, and wear gloves.

### Will call, picking, and staging

- **Pick ticket.** The warehouse picks from the pick ticket, which lists the item ID, bin, quantity, and unit. Pick in the unit on the ticket: 25 FT is 25 feet.
- **Check the pick.** Match the item ID and the bin location to the ticket. A -8 fitting and a -10 fitting look alike. The dash size is the size in sixteenths of an inch: -8 is 1/2 in and -10 is 5/8 in.
- **Stage by destination.** Branch truck orders by salesperson/route, freight and parcel orders by carrier, will-call orders in the will-call area.
- **Tag will-call orders** with the customer name, order number, and PO, then let the customer know it is ready.
- **At pickup**, confirm who is picking up and get a signature on the pick ticket or packing list.

### Vendor returns

The branch returns two kinds of product to vendors: overstock (unsold stock, usually with a restocking fee and a time limit) and defective product (through the vendor's RMA and warranty process). Purchasing arranges both. Module 15 covers returns in full.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Every step depends on the one before it being done right. A mistake in one step makes each step after more difficult and prone to subsequent errors. Do the job right every step of the way.

## 1.6 What If, Rookie Mistakes, and Safety

### What if...

| Situation | What to do | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The customer only knows "the same hose as last time" | Open Customer Sales History, find the line, and read the item and description back | The order history shows exactly what was sold |
| The item shows on hand but nothing is available | Check Open Order for what is allocated, then Open PO and Transfer for what is coming | On-hand stock that is allocated belongs to another customer |
| The account is on Hold | Take the order, tell the customer you need to check one thing, and send it to accounting; when accounting is unavailable, bring in your Manager | Only accounting or a Manager releases a hold; the customer never hears the details |
| A chemical customer says "it's just an acid" | Ask for the exact chemical, concentration, and temperature, or the SDS | Compatibility depends on all three |
| The PO lists a standard you do not recognize | Write it down and ask your Manager before you promise a date | The standard is part of the order and may need paperwork |

### Rookie mistakes

| Mistake | What it causes | The habit that prevents it |
|---|---|---|
| Quoting before the spec is complete | Wrong part, a return, and a second trip | Run STAMPED on every hose request |
| Promising the quantity on hand | Promising stock that belongs to another order | Read net quantity available |
| Ignoring the unit of measure | 100 EA when the customer wanted 100 FT | Read the unit before the quantity |
| Picking the first account that comes up | An order shipped to the wrong plant | Confirm the physical address and ship-to |
| Guessing a thread by eye | An adapter that will not seal | Measure with calipers and a thread gauge |
| Cutting a new reel when a remnant fits | Untagged drops and lost money | Check the remnant rack first and use the shortest remnant that fills the order |

### Safety

The one shop rule every trainee follows from the first day: wear safety glasses any time you cut material or operate shop equipment. Your branch may add its own rules; follow them too.

| Situation | What to do | Rule behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting hose, sheet, or belting | Safety glasses on before you start | OSHA 1910.133, eye protection |
| Operating a crimper, saw, press, or waterjet | Safety glasses on, and only after your Manager has trained you on that machine | Branch training rules |
| Working on or near a machine a customer is servicing | It must be locked out before anyone touches it | OSHA 1910.147, lockout/tagout |
| Moving reels and pallets | Use the forklift or reel stand only if you are trained and authorized | OSHA 1910.178, powered industrial trucks |
| Handling chemicals or a chemical-soaked part | Read the SDS, wear the listed protection | OSHA 1910.1200, hazard communication |

Three hazards from the products we sell come up again and again in this course:

1. **Hydraulic fluid injection.** A pinhole leak at high pressure can push oil through skin. Never use a hand to find a hydraulic leak. Pass cardboard near the hose to find the leak. Then shut down and relieve pressure before anyone touches it.
2. **Steam.** Steam service needs steam-rated hose, steam fittings, and the right clamps. A wrong substitute can burst and cause severe burns.
3. **Chemicals.** The wrong hose or gasket in chemical service can fail and release the chemical. Always confirm compatibility.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Wear safety glasses whenever you cut or operate equipment. This rule has no exceptions. The safest answer to a customer is sometimes "Let me check that with my Manager." Never guess on steam, chemicals, or high-pressure hydraulics.

## 1.8 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Module Quiz

### Practice in the P21Play System

1. **Find the customer.** Search for one of your branch's large accounts by part of its name, then by phone number. Write down the customer ID, the number of ship-tos, and the terms.
2. **Find an item.** Look up a stocked hose and a stocked fitting. Write down the net quantity available and the unit of measure for each.

4. **Walk the branch.** With your Manager, find the counter, the will-call area, the staging area, the remnant rack, and the shop. Write down who works in each.

### Knowledge check (practice)

1. A paper mill buys a replacement hose to fix a burst line on a press. Is that an MRO or an OEM purchase?
2. List two things a distributor adds that a manufacturer usually doesn't.
3. A customer's terms are "2% 15, Net 60." What does that mean?
4. Which material fails in petroleum oil: EPDM or nitrile?
5. Which ASME standard covers the flanges a standard 4 in Class 150 gasket fits?
6. An order needs 22 ft of hose. The rack holds 18 ft, 25 ft, and 30 ft remnants. Which do you cut?
7. How should you look for a leak on a pressurized hydraulic hose?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your Manager.
