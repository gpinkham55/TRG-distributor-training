An industrial rubber, hose, and gasket distributor keeps plants, farms, and equipment running by stocking the right parts close to the customer, building assemblies and cut gaskets to order, and answering technical questions fast. This module explains how the business works, who the customers are, and the four industries this course draws on.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Explain what a distributor adds between the manufacturer and the end user.
2. Describe the main customer types and how each one buys.
3. Name where our products are used in pulp and paper, agriculture, chemical manufacturing, and heavy equipment.
4. Use the basic vocabulary of the counter and the order desk.
5. Follow the shop safety basics.

## 1.1 What a Distributor Does

A distributor buys products from manufacturers in bulk, stocks them near the customer, adds value by building and cutting parts to order, and sells them in the quantities and on the timeline the customer needs.

### Where the distributor fits

| Link in the chain | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer | Makes bulk hose, fittings, gasket sheet, belting, seals | A hose mill sells hydraulic hose on 250-foot reels |
| Distributor | Stocks a wide range locally, builds and cuts to order, gives technical help, delivers | The branch cuts 6.5 feet of hose, crimps two fittings, tests it, and ships it the same day |
| End user | Installs and runs the product | A paper mill maintenance crew replaces a burst hose on a press |

Manufacturers rarely sell one hose, one gasket, or one fitting to a plant. The distributor bridges that gap.

### What a distributor adds

| Value added | What it means for the customer |
|---|---|
| Local inventory | Parts on the shelf today instead of weeks from the factory |
| Breadth | Hose, fittings, gaskets, seals, and belting from one source and one invoice |
| Fabrication | Hose assemblies, cut gaskets, and kits built to the customer's spec |
| Technical knowledge | Help choosing the right product for the pressure, temperature, and media |
| Speed | Counter pickup, local delivery, and emergency service when a line is down |
| Records | Order history, so a customer can reorder "the same as last time" |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Customers can often buy a part cheaper online. They buy from a distributor for the right part, the same day, with someone who knows what it is. Every interaction should prove that value.

### What we make

Much of a distributor's value comes from **fabrication**: turning bulk material into a finished part.

- **Hose assemblies:** bulk hose cut to length with fittings crimped or clamped on each end, then tested and tagged.
- **Cut gaskets:** gaskets cut from sheet or roll on a waterjet, a steel rule die, or by hand.
- **Kits:** several parts sold together under one item, such as a gasket set for a pump rebuild.
- **Cut-to-length goods:** hose, sheet, and O-ring cord sold by the foot or the square inch.

## 1.2 Customers and How They Buy

Distributors serve seven main customer types, and each one buys differently: what they value, who places the order, and how they pay.

| Customer type | Who places the order | What they value most | Typical request |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant maintenance (MRO) | Maintenance planner, storeroom buyer, purchasing | Speed, the right part, uptime | "Our press hose blew. We need one built today." |
| Manufacturer (OEM) | Purchasing, production planning | Consistent quality, on-time repeat deliveries, price | "Ship 2,000 of part #4471 gaskets every month." |
| Agriculture | Farm owner, operator, equipment dealer | Fast turnaround in planting and harvest, field-ready parts | "My sprayer hose split. Can you make one while I wait?" |
| Construction and equipment | Mechanic, fleet manager, contractor | Getting machines back to work, field service | "The excavator's boom hose is out on the job site." |
| Municipal and utilities | Fleet or plant supervisor, city purchasing | Correct paperwork, contract pricing, reliability | "We need a quote for the water plant on a city PO." |
| Other distributors and resellers | Buyer at the other company | Price, availability, fast shipping | "Do you have 100 feet of 2-inch suction hose in stock?" |
| Walk-in and cash customers | Anyone at the counter | Speed and a straight answer | "Can you match this fitting?" |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: **MRO** (maintenance, repair, and operations) customers buy to keep equipment running, often in a hurry. **OEM** (original equipment manufacturer) customers buy parts that go into the products they build, usually on a schedule. Most branches serve both, and the order desk handles them very differently.

### How requests arrive

| Channel | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Phone call | The most common channel for urgent requests; the caller often describes the part, not the part number |
| Text message | Quick photos of a fitting or a hose layline; confirm the details before you act |
| Email | Purchase orders, quote requests, drawings, and spec sheets |
| Counter walk-in | The customer brings the old part; measure it, identify it, and match it |
| Outside sales rep | The rep brings in a quote request or order from a customer visit |

Every request, from any channel, follows the same path: identify the customer, understand the need, find or build the part, and confirm price, quantity, and delivery.

### How customers pay

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Purchase order (PO) | The customer's written authority to buy; most businesses require one on every order and invoice |
| Blanket PO and release | One PO covers many orders over time; each shipment carries a release number |
| Net terms (Net 30, Net 60) | The invoice is due 30 or 60 days after it's issued |
| Discount terms (2% 15, Net 60) | 2% off if paid within 15 days, otherwise due in 60 |
| COD (cash on delivery) | Paid at pickup or delivery, usually by card or cash |
| Credit card | Paid at the time of the order |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never ship to a customer whose account is on credit hold without approval from your manager or accounting. Take the order, explain the hold politely, and route it.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: A down machine can cost a plant or a farmer thousands of dollars an hour. Speed and accuracy on urgent requests are what customers remember.

## 1.3 The Four Industries

This course draws its examples from four industries that use large amounts of hose, gaskets, seals, and belting. Each one has its own conditions, schedules, and vocabulary.

| Industry | Operating conditions | Busiest times | What a down part costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulp and paper | Steam, hot water, caustic and bleach chemicals, abrasive slurries; runs around the clock | Planned shutdowns (outages), when hundreds of parts are replaced at once | Lost production on a machine that never normally stops |
| Agriculture | Water, fertilizer and chemical sprays, grain dust, hydraulics in the field, weather | Planting and harvest seasons | A missed planting or harvest window |
| Chemical manufacturing | Acids, caustics, solvents, high temperatures; strict safety and environmental rules | Planned turnarounds and maintenance windows | Safety exposure, product loss, and regulatory reporting |
| Heavy equipment and construction | High-pressure hydraulics, vibration, dirt, impact, extreme temperatures | Construction season; any day a machine breaks down | An idle machine and crew on a job site |

### Pulp and paper

A pulp and paper mill turns wood chips into pulp, then into paper or paperboard. The process uses large volumes of steam, water, and chemicals, and the machines run continuously.

| Area of the mill | Products used |
|---|---|
| Digester and pulping (cooking chips with chemicals) | Steam hose, high-temperature gaskets, expansion joints |
| Bleach plant | Chemical-resistant hose, PTFE and ePTFE gaskets |
| Recovery boiler and power house | Steam hose, flexible graphite gaskets, metal hose |
| Paper machine | Water and wash-down hose, rubber seals, belting, custom gaskets |
| Wood yard and material handling | Conveyor belting, abrasive slurry hose |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A paper machine wet end with water spray lines and hoses along the frame]

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Mills plan **outages** months ahead. A buyer may ask for a quote on 200 gaskets with pricing held for 60 days. Accuracy and lead time on outage quotes win the next year's business.

### Agriculture

Farms, ranches, and their equipment dealers run on water, chemicals, and hydraulics, often far from a parts counter.

| Application | Products used |
|---|---|
| Irrigation | Suction and discharge hose, layflat hose, pipe gaskets, cam and groove couplings |
| Crop spraying | Chemical-resistant spray hose, fittings, pump gaskets |
| Tractors, combines, and implements | Hydraulic hose assemblies, drive belts, seals, O-rings |
| Grain handling | Conveyor belting, material handling hose |
| Livestock and wash-down | Water hose, nozzles, hose reels |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A center-pivot irrigation system with the pump station, suction hose, and discharge lines in view]

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Agriculture is seasonal. A broken hose during planting or harvest is an emergency, and the farmer often drives in with the failed part in hand.

### Chemical manufacturing

Chemical plants make and move hazardous liquids and gases. Every product that touches the media must be compatible with it, and plants follow strict safety rules.

| Application | Products used |
|---|---|
| Unloading tank trucks and railcars | Chemical transfer hose, PTFE-lined hose, cam and groove couplings |
| Process piping | PTFE, ePTFE, and FKM (Viton type) gaskets; expansion joints |
| Pumps and valves | Mechanical seals, O-rings, packing |
| Tanks and vessels | Manway gaskets, lined-vessel gaskets |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A chemical tank truck unloading station with a PTFE-lined hose and a dry-disconnect coupling]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: In chemical service, never guess compatibility. Ask for the exact chemical, concentration, and temperature, and check the manufacturer's chemical resistance chart. A wrong guess can cause a leak, an injury, or a release.

### Heavy equipment and construction

Excavators, loaders, dozers, cranes, and trucks depend on high-pressure hydraulics and take constant vibration and abuse.

| Application | Products used |
|---|---|
| Booms, buckets, and steering | Spiral and braided hydraulic hose assemblies, adapters |
| Engines and cabs | Engine mounts, vibration isolators, cab seals, gaskets |
| Undercarriage and tracks | Seals, wear parts, hydraulic fittings |
| Fuel and fluid service | Fuel and oil hose, fittings, hose reels |
| Dust control and dewatering | Water hose, suction and discharge hose |

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: An excavator boom with its hydraulic hoses and fittings labeled]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Hydraulic systems run at thousands of psi. A pinhole leak can inject fluid through skin and cause a serious injury. Never run a hand along a pressurized hose to find a leak.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: The same products show up in all four industries. What changes is the media, the pressure, the temperature, and the urgency. Learn to ask about all four on every request.

## 1.5 Safety Basics

The one shop rule every trainee follows from the first day: wear safety glasses any time you cut material or operate shop equipment. Your branch may add its own rules; follow them too.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Cutting hose, sheet, or belting | Safety glasses on before you start |
| Operating a crimper, saw, press, or waterjet | Safety glasses on, and only after your mentor has trained you on that machine |
| Walking through the shop | Stay clear of machines in use and of anyone cutting |
| Handling a customer's failed hose | Treat it as dirty and possibly pressurized or chemical-soaked until you know otherwise |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Wear safety glasses whenever you cut or operate equipment. It is the one rule with no exceptions.

Three hazards from the products we sell come up again and again in this course. Know them now:

1. **Hydraulic fluid injection.** A pinhole leak at high pressure can push oil through skin. Never use a hand to find a hydraulic leak.
2. **Steam.** Steam hose needs steam-rated hose, steam fittings, and the right clamps. A wrong substitute can burst and cause severe burns.
3. **Chemicals.** The wrong hose or gasket in chemical service can fail and release the chemical. Always confirm compatibility.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: The safest answer to a customer is sometimes "Let me check that with my mentor." Never guess on steam, chemicals, or high-pressure hydraulics.
