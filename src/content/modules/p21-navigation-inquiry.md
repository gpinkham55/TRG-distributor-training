Prophet 21 (P21) is the system every branch uses to look up customers and items, enter orders, buy stock, and track every sale. Before you enter anything, you need to find your way around and look things up fast. This module teaches the menus and windows, then the three lookups you will use on almost every call and at the counter: the customer, the item, and the customer's past orders.

### Learning objectives

After this module you will be able to:

1. Sign in to the correct system and tell the P21Play System from live P21.
2. Open windows from the menu, search for records, move between tabs, and keep more than one window open.
3. Tell an inquiry window from a maintenance window, and use the right one.
4. Find a customer and read its account, terms, notes, ship-tos, and contacts.
5. Find an item and read its unit of measure, stock by location, open purchase orders, and the customer's price.
6. Find what a customer bought before using sales history.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Window and field labels in this module follow standard P21. Each branch can rename fields, hide tabs, and add custom fields. Your mentor will point out where your branch's screens differ. Write those differences in your notebook.

## 2.1 Live P21 and the P21Play System

TRG runs two copies of P21. **Live P21** is the real system: orders entered there ship, print, and bill. The **P21Play System** is a copy of live data used for training. Every practice task in this course goes in the P21Play System, never in live P21, until your mentor moves you over.

|  | Live P21 | P21Play System |
|---|---|---|
| What it holds | Real customers, items, orders, and stock | A copy of live data, refreshed from time to time |
| What happens when you save | Orders print in the warehouse, POs go to vendors, invoices go to customers | Nothing leaves the building |
| When you use it | After your mentor signs you off on that task | All practice in this course |

Stock and order status in the P21Play System may not match live exactly. When an exercise and the P21Play System disagree, trust the P21Play System and note what you found.

### Signing in

1. Open P21 from the shortcut your branch set up. You may see a list of systems or databases; pick the P21Play System for practice.
2. Sign in with **your own user ID and password**. Never use a shared login. Every order, quote, and change carries the ID of the person who made it.
3. Check the **title bar** or status bar for the system name before you start. Make this a habit every time you sign in.
4. Sign out or lock your screen when you step away.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: The P21 sign-in window. The title shows which system you are signing in to; this one says LIVE]

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Check which system you are in before you save anything. An order saved in live P21 by mistake can ship product and bill a customer for something they never ordered.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Your user ID is your signature in P21. Use only your own, and never share your password.

## 2.2 Finding Your Way Around P21

P21 is a set of windows grouped by area on the main menu. You open a window, find a record, read its tabs, and save if you changed anything.

### The main menu

The areas you will use most in your first weeks:

| Menu area | Windows you will use | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| Order processing | Order Entry, Quote Entry, Order Inquiry | Enter and look up orders and quotes |
| Customers | Customer Master Inquiry, Ship To Maintenance, Contact Maintenance | Look up accounts; keep ship-tos and contacts current |
| Inventory | Item Master Inquiry, item availability by location | Look up items, stock, and price |
| Purchasing | Purchase Order Entry, Purchase Order Inquiry | Buy stock and check what is coming (Module 13) |

Add the windows you use every day to your **favorites** or toolbar so you can open them in one click.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: The P21 menu open over Order Entry. Starred windows are your favorites]

### Searching for a record

Most ID fields (customer, ship-to, contact, item, order) open a search window.

1. Type part of the value, then press Tab or click the lookup button.
2. Search on the **shortest piece that is unique**. "RIVERB" finds more than "Riverbend Paper Company, Inc." and catches spelling differences.
3. If too many records come back, add a second filter such as city, phone, or ZIP code.
4. Pick the record, then confirm it is the right one before you go on.

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: When a search finds nothing, search again with less. Most "not in the system" answers come from searching on too much text.

### Tabs

Most windows have tabs across the top. The first tab holds the main record. The other tabs hold related detail: ship-tos, contacts, notes, history, open orders. The first time you open each window, click through every tab and note what lives where.

### Inquiry windows and maintenance windows

| Window type | What it does | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Inquiry | Shows a record; you cannot change it | You only need to look |
| Maintenance | Adds or changes a record | You need to add or fix something, and you are trained to |
| Entry | Creates a transaction (order, quote, PO) | You are entering a new transaction |

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Use the inquiry window when you only need to look. A maintenance window opened "just to check" is the most common way trainees change a record by accident.

### More than one window

You can keep several windows open at once. Keep Customer Master Inquiry open beside Order Entry so you can check history and notes as you take an order.

### Saving and closing

Changes are not kept until you save. If you close a window with unsaved changes, P21 asks what to do. Read the prompt before you answer it.

### Notes and pop-ups

Customers, ship-tos, items, and orders can carry notes. A note set to pop up appears when you open the record. Read every one. It may say "PO required," "call before delivery," or "do not substitute brands."

### Common mistakes

- Working in live P21 when you meant the P21Play System.
- Opening a maintenance window to look and saving a change by accident.
- Clicking past a pop-up note without reading it.
- Searching on a full name, finding nothing, and assuming the record is missing.

## 2.3 Customer Inquiry

Before you quote, enter an order, or answer a question, know who the customer is, how they buy, and what rules come with the account. **Customer Master Inquiry** shows it all in one place.

### How customer records fit together

| Record | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Customer (bill-to) | The account that gets the invoice | Riverbend Paper Company, billed to its accounts payable office |
| Ship-to | A delivery address under the customer | The mill, a warehouse across town, a second plant |
| Contact | A person at the customer | A buyer, a maintenance planner, an accounts payable clerk |

One customer can have many ship-tos and many contacts. Some companies have more than one customer account, for example one per plant, each billing to a different office. Module 3 teaches how to add and maintain ship-tos and contacts.

### Steps

1. Open **Customer Master Inquiry**.
2. Search by customer ID, part of the name, phone number, or city.
3. **Confirm the right account.** Check the bill-to address and the ship-to list before you go on. If two accounts look alike, ask the caller which plant or address they are calling about.
4. Read the **terms** and the account status. An account on hold or set to cash only changes what you can promise; ask your mentor before you go on.
5. Read the **notes**: PO required, release numbers, receiving hours, tax exemption, brand preferences.
6. Look at the **ship-tos** and **contacts**. Is the caller listed? Is the delivery address on file?
7. Note the **salesperson** on the account so you know who to keep informed.
8. Check **open orders** and **open quotes** before you create anything new. A customer calling to "order those gaskets" may already have a quote waiting.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Customer Master Inquiry with the bill-to address, terms, salesperson, and the Ship To and Contacts tabs marked]

### Fields to check every time

| Field | Why you check it |
|---|---|
| Customer ID | Every order, quote, and invoice is tied to it |
| Bill-to address | Confirms you have the right account |
| Terms | How and when the customer pays |
| Account status | A hold or cash-only status changes what you can promise |
| Notes | Customer rules you must follow on every order |
| Customer part number | Lets you enter the customer's own part number and find your item |
| Salesperson | Who owns the relationship |

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Large customers often have many accounts. A paper company can have one account per mill; an equipment dealer can have one per store. The name alone rarely tells you which one. The bill-to address and ship-to list do.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Never tell a customer their credit limit, balance, or account status over the phone or at the counter. If an account is on hold, say you need to check one thing and bring in your mentor or manager.

### Common mistakes

- Picking the first account that comes up without checking the bill-to.
- Skipping the notes, then missing a required PO or release number.
- Asking for a new account when the customer is already in P21 under another spelling. Search by phone and city before you decide.
- Creating a new quote when an open quote already covers the request.

## 2.4 Item Inquiry and Stock by Location

Most requests come down to three questions: do you have it, where is it, and what does it cost? **Item Master Inquiry** answers all three.

### Steps

1. Open **Item Master Inquiry**.
2. Search by item ID, part of the description, a manufacturer part number, or the customer's part number. Your branch's item ID format makes searching much faster; your mentor will show you how IDs are built.
3. Read the full **description** and the **unit of measure** (UOM).
4. Check stock at **your location**: on hand, allocated, and available.
5. Check **other locations**. Another branch may have it and can transfer it to you.
6. Check **open purchase orders**: how many are coming and the expected date.
7. Check the **price for this customer**. Enter the customer ID so the customer's own pricing applies, not a list price.
8. Look at **recent sales** of the item to see who buys it and in what quantities.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: Item Master Inquiry showing the item description, unit of measure, and the location tab with on hand, allocated, and available columns]

### Reading stock quantities

| Quantity | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| On hand | Physically in your building | 100 ft |
| Allocated | Already promised to open orders | 60 ft |
| Available | On hand minus allocated; what you can promise | 40 ft |
| On order | Coming in on open purchase orders | 200 ft, expected in 5 business days |

If a customer needs 50 ft in this example, you can ship 40 ft now. Offer the other 10 ft from another location, or from the open purchase order.

> ⚠️ SAFETY & COMPLIANCE: Promise only the **available** quantity. Stock that is on hand but allocated belongs to another customer's order.

### Units of measure

The unit of measure tells you what one unit of the item is. Read it every time before you enter a quantity.

| Unit | Meaning | Typical items |
|---|---|---|
| EA | Each, one piece | Fittings, clamps, couplings, cut gaskets |
| FT | Foot | Bulk hose, tubing, cord |
| SI | Square inch | Gasket sheet sold by the piece |
| RL | Roll | Full rolls of sheet rubber or tape |
| BX, PK | Box, pack | O-ring kits, small parts sold in packs |

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: 100 SI of gasket sheet is a piece about 10 in x 10 in, not 100 sheets. Read the unit before you read the quantity.

### When you can't find it

1. Search again with less text, or with a manufacturer or customer part number.
2. Check whether the item goes by another description (for example, "cam lock" for a cam and groove fitting).
3. Ask your mentor. The item may need to be special ordered or set up as a new item; Module 6 covers both.

### Common mistakes

- Promising on-hand stock that is already allocated.
- Reading the wrong unit of measure.
- Telling a customer "we don't have it" without checking other locations and open purchase orders.
- Quoting price without the customer ID, so the customer's pricing doesn't apply.

## 2.5 Sales History and Past Orders

"Send me the same hose as last time" is one of the most common requests you will get. The customer rarely knows the part number. **Sales history** is how you find it.

### Where to look

| What you need | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Everything a customer bought in a date range | The customer's sales history (invoice line history) |
| The full order: every line, the PO, the ship-to | Order Inquiry, opened from the history line |
| Orders not yet shipped | The customer's open orders |
| Prices given but not yet ordered | The customer's open quotes |
| Who else buys an item | The item's sales history |

### Steps: "the same as last time"

1. Find the customer and confirm the account (2.3).
2. Open the customer's **sales history**.
3. Narrow the list with what the customer gives you: a **date range** ("back in April"), a word from the **description** ("steam," "2 inch"), the **ship-to** ("for the No. 2 machine"), or their **PO number**.
4. Open the matching line and read the **item ID**, **quantity**, **unit of measure**, and **price**.
5. Open the **order** to see the other lines on it. A hose often ships with fittings, clamps, or a matching assembly on the same order.
6. Read the item description back to the customer to confirm. Ask whether anything has changed: length, end fittings, temperature, or media.

[IMAGE EXAMPLE: A customer's sales history filtered by date range, with one hose line selected and the order number visible]

> 💡 KEY TAKEAWAY: Sales history answers "what did we sell them" with facts. Use it before you ask the customer to describe the part again.

> 📌 INDUSTRY INSIGHT: Plants often buy the same parts every outage or every season. A quick look at what a customer bought this time last year can tell you what they will need next.

### Common mistakes

- Searching history on the wrong account and finding nothing.
- Copying the item but not the unit of measure.
- Missing the fittings or clamps that shipped on the same order.
- Quoting last year's price without checking the current price for the customer.

## 2.6 Practice, Knowledge Check, and Test-Out

### Practice in the P21Play System

Your mentor picks three real accounts and three items for these exercises. Write your answers in your notebook and bring them to your mentor.

1. **Navigation.** Add Customer Master Inquiry, Item Master Inquiry, Order Inquiry, and Order Entry to your favorites. Open each one and list its tabs.
2. **Customer lookup.** For each of the three accounts, find and write down: customer ID, bill-to city, terms, salesperson, number of ship-tos, and any notes that pop up.
3. **Two accounts, one company.** Your mentor gives you a company with more than one account. Find each account and write what makes them different.
4. **Item lookup.** For each of the three items, write: item ID, description, unit of measure, available quantity at your location, the location with the most stock, and quantity on open purchase orders.
5. **Customer price.** Look up the price of one item for two different customers. Note whether the prices differ.
6. **Same as last time.** Your mentor plays a customer: "I need the same hose you sent us a few months back." Find the item, quantity, unit, PO, and order number in sales history.

### Knowledge check (practice, not graded)

1. You want to check a customer's terms. Do you open an inquiry window or a maintenance window?
2. A search for "Riverbend Paper Company Inc" finds nothing. What do you try next?
3. An item shows 120 on hand and 90 allocated. How many can you promise a new order?
4. A customer orders 300 SI of gasket sheet. About how big a piece is that?
5. A caller says, "Send me what we got back in March." Where do you look first?
6. Name two places to check before you tell a customer "we don't have it."
7. How do you confirm you picked the right account when a company has three?

Work these on your own, then check your answers with your mentor.

### Module quiz and test-out

- **Module quiz:** 10 questions drawn from a pool of 25; 80% to pass.
- **Test-out:** 10 questions; 90% to pass. Passing marks Module 2's reading complete. The mentor sign-off below still applies.
- **Mentor sign-off:** with your mentor watching, find a customer, an item, and a past order on your own, using only the information a caller would give you.
